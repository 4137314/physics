#+title: AC power and power factor
#+summary: With rms phasors, complex power \(S=\mathbf V\mathbf I^*=P+jQ\): active power \(P=V_{rms}I_{rms}\cos\varphi\) (W) does work, reactive power \(Q=V_{rms}I_{rms}\sin\varphi\) (var) shuttles energy in and out of L and C, apparent power \(|S|\) (VA) sizes the equipment; power factor \(\cos\varphi\) is corrected by adding the opposite reactance.

* Definition and derivation
\(p(t)=v(t)i(t)=V_mI_m\cos(\omega t)\cos(\omega t-\varphi)=\frac{V_mI_m}{2}[\cos\varphi+\cos(2\omega t-\varphi)]\). Average \(P=\frac{V_mI_m}2\cos\varphi=V_{rms}I_{rms}\cos\varphi\). Complex power conserves around a network (Tellegen): \(\sum S_k=0\) [cite:@steinmetz1893].
| element | P   | Q        |
|---------+-----+----------|
| R       | I²R | 0        |
| L       | 0   | +I²ωL    |
| C       | 0   | −I²/(ωC) |

* Power-factor correction
Load \(P\) at angle \(\varphi_1\); target \(\varphi_2\): capacitor reactive power \(Q_C=P(\tan\varphi_1-\tan\varphi_2)\), \(C=\frac{Q_C}{\omega V_{rms}^2}\). Benefit: lower line current \(I=P/(V\cos\varphi)\), hence lower losses \(I^2r\) ([[id:power-generation-and-distribution][distribution]]).

* Worked example
Motor 10 kW at PF 0.7 lagging on 230 V, 50 Hz: \(I=10000/(230\cdot0.7)=62\) A, \(Q=10\tan45.6^\circ=10.2\) kvar. Correct to 0.95: \(Q_C=10(1.02-0.329)=6.9\) kvar, \(C=\frac{6900}{2\pi50\cdot230^2}=415\) µF; new current 45.8 A — line losses drop by 45%.

* Engineering applications
Industrial PF correction (utilities charge for reactive energy), active PFC in switch-mode supplies (EU harmonic limits), inverter sizing in VA, reactive-power control for grid voltage support.

* Pitfall
PF also includes harmonic distortion: a rectifier load with in-phase but distorted current has PF < 1 even with \(\varphi=0\) (distortion factor).
