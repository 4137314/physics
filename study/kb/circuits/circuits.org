#+title: Circuit theory: the map of the subject
#+summary: Lumped circuits (size ≪ wavelength) obey Kirchhoff's laws; linear time-invariant networks are fully described by their impulse response or, equivalently, by a transfer function analysed with phasors (sinusoids), Fourier (spectra) and Laplace (transients); transformers and three-phase systems carry the power.

* Overview
| block       | notes                                                 |
|-------------+-------------------------------------------------------|
| foundations | lumped elements and Kirchhoff, network theorems       |
| time domain | LTI systems, impulse response, convolution, RC/RL/RLC |
| AC          | complex numbers and phasors, impedance, AC power      |
| transforms  | Fourier series/transform, frequency response, Laplace |
| power       | transformer, generation and three-phase distribution  |
| analog      | operational-amplifier circuits                        |

* Method selection
| question                            | tool                             |
|-------------------------------------+----------------------------------|
| DC operating point                  | KCL/KVL, Thévenin, superposition |
| sinusoidal steady state             | phasors and impedances           |
| response to periodic signal         | Fourier series × H(jω)           |
| transient after a switch, stability | Laplace transform, poles         |
| arbitrary input, time domain        | convolution with h(t)            |

* Engineering applications
Every electronic system: filters, amplifiers, power supplies, grids, sensors, the small-signal models of [[id:digital-electronics][transistors]] and the lumped limit of [[id:transmission-lines][transmission lines]].
