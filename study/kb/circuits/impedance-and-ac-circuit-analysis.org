#+title: Impedance and the symbolic method
#+summary: With phasors, \(Z_R=R\), \(Z_L=j\omega L\), \(Z_C=\frac{1}{j\omega C}\); impedances combine like resistances (series add, parallel admittances add), all DC theorems hold, and a network function \(H(j\omega)=\mathbf V_{out}/\mathbf V_{in}\) describes the circuit; RLC resonance at \(\omega_0=1/\sqrt{LC}\) with \(Q=\omega_0L/R\) (series) and bandwidth \(\omega_0/Q\).

* Method
1. Draw the phasor circuit: each element → impedance.
2. Apply series/parallel reduction, dividers, nodal or mesh analysis, Thévenin — exactly as for resistors, with complex arithmetic.
3. Network function \(H(j\omega)\): magnitude = gain, argument = phase shift.
Admittance \(Y=1/Z=G+jB\). \(Z=R+jX\): \(X>0\) inductive (current lags), \(X<0\) capacitive (current leads).

* Resonance
Series RLC: \(Z=R+j(\omega L-\frac1{\omega C})\), minimum \(|Z|=R\) at \(\omega_0\); voltages across \(L\) and \(C\) are \(Q\) times the source. Parallel RLC: maximum impedance \(R\) at \(\omega_0\), \(Q=R/\omega_0L\). Half-power bandwidth \(\Delta\omega=\omega_0/Q\).

* Worked example
Series \(R=10\) Ω, \(L=10\) mH, \(C=1\) µF, source 1 V: \(\omega_0=10^4\) rad/s (1.59 kHz), \(Q=\frac{10^4\cdot0.01}{10}=10\), bandwidth 159 Hz. At resonance \(I=0.1\) A and \(|V_L|=|V_C|=\omega_0LI=10\) V — ten times the source.

* Engineering applications
Tuned circuits in radios and oscillators, impedance of decoupling networks, sensor excitation, [[id:smith-chart-and-impedance-matching][matching networks]], power-factor correction ([[id:ac-power-and-power-factor][AC power]]), LC filters in switching converters.

* Pitfall
At resonance the source sees a pure resistance, but element voltages (series) or currents (parallel) can be dangerously large: component ratings must consider \(Q\).
