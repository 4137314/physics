#+title: The Laplace transform and circuit transients
#+summary: \(X(s)=\int_{0^-}^\infty x(t)e^{-st}\dd t\) turns differential equations with initial conditions into algebra: \(\mathcal L[\dot x]=sX-x(0^-)\); impedances become \(R\), \(sL\), \(1/sC\) (with initial-condition sources), the transfer function \(H(s)\) has poles whose real parts decide stability, and partial fractions give the time response.

* Key transforms and theorems
| x(t)                | X(s)                     |
|---------------------+--------------------------|
| δ(t)                | 1                        |
| u(t)                | 1/s                      |
| e^{−at} u(t)        | 1/(s + a)                |
| t e^{−at} u(t)      | 1/(s + a)²               |
| sin ω₀t u(t)        | ω₀/(s² + ω₀²)            |
| e^{−at}cos ω₀t u(t) | (s + a)/((s + a)² + ω₀²) |
Initial value \(x(0^+)=\lim_{s\to\infty}sX(s)\); final value \(x(\infty)=\lim_{s\to0}sX(s)\) (only if poles of \(sX\) are in the left half-plane) [cite:@heaviside1893]. \(H(j\omega)=H(s)|_{s=j\omega}\) for stable systems.

* Method
1. Determine \(v_C(0^-)\), \(i_L(0^-)\).
2. Transform the circuit: \(L\to sL\) in series with a source \(Li_L(0^-)\) (opposing), \(C\to\frac1{sC}\) in series with \(v_C(0^-)/s\).
3. Solve algebraically for \(X(s)\).
4. Partial fractions: \(X=\sum\frac{r_k}{s-p_k}\), \(r_k=\lim_{s\to p_k}(s-p_k)X\); invert term by term.

* Worked example
RC charged to \(V_0=5\) V discharging into \(R\) while a step \(V_s=10\) V is applied at \(t=0\) (series RC to the source): \(I(s)=\frac{V_s/s-V_0/s}{R+1/sC}=\frac{(V_s-V_0)/R}{s+1/RC}\) → \(i=\frac{5}{R}e^{-t/RC}\), \(v_C=10-5e^{-t/RC}\). Final value: \(\lim s\cdot V_C(s)=10\) V ✓.

* Stability
Poles in the left half-plane → decaying modes; on the axis → oscillation; right half-plane → instability. Feedback amplifiers and oscillators are designed by placing poles (root locus, Nyquist).

* Engineering applications
Switching transients (inrush, ringing), control-loop design of power converters and PLLs, step response specs (rise time, overshoot) of amplifiers, transient analysis in SPICE, the Green's-function view of [[id:green-functions-and-feynman-propagator][propagators]].

* Pitfall
The final-value theorem gives nonsense for oscillating or unstable systems (e.g. \(\sin t\)): check pole locations first.
