#+title: The transformer
#+summary: An ideal transformer (no losses, no leakage, infinite self-inductance) obeys \(\frac{v_2}{v_1}=\frac{n_2}{n_1}\), \(\frac{i_2}{i_1}=-\frac{n_1}{n_2}\) (associated reference directions), conserves power and reflects a load as \(Z_{in}=\left(\frac{n_1}{n_2}\right)^2Z_L\); a real transformer adds magnetising and leakage inductances, winding resistances and core losses.

* Ideal model (derivation)
Same flux \(\Phi\) links both windings (no leakage): \(v_1=n_1\dot\Phi\), \(v_2=n_2\dot\Phi\Rightarrow v_2/v_1=n_2/n_1\). Ampère around the core: \(n_1i_1+n_2i_2=\mathcal R\Phi\to0\) for infinite permeability (infinite self-inductance): \(i_2/i_1=-n_1/n_2\) [cite:@faraday1832]. Hence \(v_1i_1+v_2i_2=0\): no stored or dissipated energy. With load \(Z_L\) on the secondary (\(\mathbf V_2=-Z_L\mathbf I_2\)): \(Z_{in}=\frac{\mathbf V_1}{\mathbf I_1}=\left(\frac{n_1}{n_2}\right)^2Z_L\).

* Coupled inductors and real transformer
\(v_1=L_1\dot i_1+M\dot i_2\), \(v_2=M\dot i_1+L_2\dot i_2\), \(M=k\sqrt{L_1L_2}\). Equivalent circuit: \(R_1\), leakage \(L_{\sigma1}\), magnetising \(L_m\) in parallel with core-loss \(R_c\), ideal transformer, \(R_2'\), \(L_{\sigma2}'\). Open-circuit test → \(L_m,R_c\); short-circuit test → \(R_{eq},L_{\sigma}\). Efficiency of large units > 99%.

* Worked example
Audio output: 8 Ω speaker to look like 800 Ω for a tube amplifier: \((n_1/n_2)^2=100\), turns ratio 10:1. Mains transformer 230 V → 12 V, 2 A secondary: \(n_1/n_2=19.2\), primary current ≈ 0.104 A plus magnetising current (lagging, typically 2–10% of rated).

* Engineering applications
Voltage conversion and isolation in power grids and supplies ([[id:power-generation-and-distribution][distribution]]), flyback/forward converters ([[id:switching-converters][switching converters]]), impedance matching (baluns, RF transformers), current transformers for metering, galvanic isolation in gate drivers.

* Pitfall
Transformers do not work at DC: a DC component saturates the core (\(L_m\) collapses). And the ideal current relation fails at light load, where the magnetising current dominates.
