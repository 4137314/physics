#+title: Power dissipation in CMOS
#+summary: Each full charge–discharge cycle of a load \(C\) dissipates \(CV_{DD}^2\) (half in the PMOS while charging, half in the NMOS while discharging, independent of resistances), so \(P_{dyn}=\alpha CV_{DD}^2f\); add short-circuit power during slow transitions and leakage \(V_{DD}I_{leak}\); the power–delay product \(PDP\approx CV_{DD}^2\) is the energy per operation.

* Derivation of \(CV_{DD}^2\)
Charging: the supply delivers \(Q=CV_{DD}\) at voltage \(V_{DD}\), energy \(CV_{DD}^2\); the capacitor stores \(\tfrac12CV_{DD}^2\); the other half is dissipated in the PMOS (any resistance: \(\int i^2R\,\dd t=\tfrac12CV^2\) for an RC charge). Discharging: the stored \(\tfrac12CV_{DD}^2\) is dissipated in the NMOS. Per cycle \(CV_{DD}^2\); with transition probability \(\alpha\) (0→1 transitions per clock), \(P=\alpha CV_{DD}^2f\).

* Other components
- *Short-circuit*: both devices on while \(V_{TN}<v_I<V_{DD}-|V_{TP}|\); \(P_{sc}\approx\frac{K}{12}(V_{DD}-2V_T)^3\frac{t_r}{T}\) (Veendrick) — small if input and output edges are similar.
- *Leakage*: subthreshold, gate tunnelling, junction: \(P=V_{DD}I_{leak}\), exponential in \(-V_T\) and in temperature.

* Reduction techniques
| technique                   | acts on               |
|-----------------------------+-----------------------|
| lower V_DD (DVFS)           | V² (and f)            |
| clock gating                | α                     |
| smaller capacitance, sizing | C                     |
| power gating, multi-V_T     | leakage               |
| parallelism at lower V_DD   | V² at same throughput |

* Worked example
Chip with \(10^8\) gates, average \(C=1\) fF, \(V_{DD}=1\) V, \(f=2\) GHz, \(\alpha=0.1\): \(P=0.1\cdot10^{-7}\cdot1\cdot2\times10^9=20\) W. Lowering \(V_{DD}\) to 0.8 V: 12.8 W (−36%). An inverter driving 100 fF at 2.5 V and 100 MHz with \(\alpha=0.5\): \(0.5\cdot10^{-13}\cdot6.25\cdot10^8=31\) µW; Energy per full cycle \(CV_{DD}^2=0.625\) pJ, i.e. \(\tfrac12CV_{DD}^2=0.31\) pJ per transition — the power–delay product of this gate.

* Engineering applications
Battery life of mobile SoCs, thermal design power, near-threshold computing for IoT, data-centre efficiency; DRAM refresh and SRAM leakage budgets ([[id:sram-cell][SRAM]], [[id:dram-cell][DRAM]]).

* Pitfall
\(\alpha\) is defined in different ways (per clock cycle, 0→1 only, or any transition); with "any transition" the formula becomes \(\tfrac12\alpha CV^2f\). State the convention.
