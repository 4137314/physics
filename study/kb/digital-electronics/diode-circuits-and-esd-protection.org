#+title: Diode circuits and ESD protection
#+summary: Diode circuits are solved by guessing on/off states with a model (ideal, constant drop \(V_{on}\approx0.7\) V, piecewise linear with \(r_d\)) or by iterating the Shockley equation; CMOS inputs are protected against electrostatic discharge by diodes to \(V_{DD}\) and ground that clamp the gate within one diode drop of the rails.

* Method
1. Assume a state for each diode (on: \(V=V_{on}\), \(I>0\); off: \(I=0\), \(V<V_{on}\)).
2. Solve the linear circuit.
3. Check consistency; if violated, flip the assumption.
For accuracy use the Shockley law \(I=I_s(e^{V/nV_T}-1)\) with a load line \(I=(V_S-V)/R\), iterating \(V\leftarrow nV_T\ln(I/I_s)\).

* ESD protection
A human body charged to ~2 kV (Human Body Model: 100 pF through 1.5 kΩ, peak ≈ 1.3 A, ~150 ns) would rupture a 2 nm gate oxide (breakdown ≈ 2–3 V). Input pads have diodes D1 (pad → \(V_{DD}\)) and D2 (ground → pad), plus a series resistor and rail clamp: the pad voltage is held within \([-V_{on},V_{DD}+V_{on}]\) and the discharge current flows into the supply network.

* Worked example
\(V_S=5\) V, \(R=1\) kΩ, diode \(I_s=10^{-14}\) A: start \(V=0.7\) → \(I=4.3\) mA → \(V=0.0259\ln(4.3\times10^{11})=0.698\) V → \(I=4.30\) mA: converged. HBM through the clamp: 1.3 A in a diode with \(r_d\approx2\) Ω gives \(V\approx0.8+2.6=3.4\) V above the rail — hence additional rail clamps and series \(R_s\).

* Engineering applications
Rectifiers and peak detectors, clamps and level shifters, reverse-polarity protection, ESD networks on every IC pad, TVS diodes on connectors (USB, Ethernet).

* Pitfall
The constant-drop model hides the temperature coefficient (−2 mV/K) and the logarithmic dependence on current; at µA currents a Si diode drops only ≈ 0.4–0.5 V.
