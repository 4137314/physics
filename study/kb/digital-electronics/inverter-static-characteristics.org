#+title: Inverter static characteristics and noise margins
#+summary: The voltage transfer characteristic \(v_O(v_I)\) defines \(V_{OH}\), \(V_{OL}\) and the unity-gain points \(V_{IL}\), \(V_{IH}\) (\(\dd v_O/\dd v_I=-1\)); noise margins \(NM_H=V_{OH}-V_{IH}\) and \(NM_L=V_{IL}-V_{OL}\) measure how much noise a chain of gates tolerates; a gain larger than 1 in the transition region restores degraded levels.

* Definitions
- \(V_{OH}\), \(V_{OL}\): output levels for input low/high (driving an identical gate).
- \(V_{IL}\), \(V_{IH}\): input boundaries where the VTC slope is −1; inputs below \(V_{IL}\) are surely "0", above \(V_{IH}\) surely "1".
- Switching threshold \(V_M\): \(v_O=v_I\).
- Noise margins as above; ideal inverter: \(V_{OH}=V_{DD}\), \(V_{OL}=0\), \(V_{IL}=V_{IH}=V_{DD}/2\), \(NM=V_{DD}/2\).

* Why slope −1
If \(|\dd v_O/\dd v_I|>1\) between \(V_{IL}\) and \(V_{IH}\), small disturbances grow there; outside, they are attenuated. Choosing the −1 points maximises the guaranteed margin for a chain of identical gates (regeneration).

* Worked example
Measured CMOS VTC (\(V_{DD}=5\) V): slope −1 at \(V_{IL}=2.8\) V (\(V_{OH}\approx4.6\) V) and \(V_{IH}=3.9\) V (\(V_{OL}\approx0.2\) V). \(NM_H=4.6-3.9=0.7\) V, \(NM_L=2.8-0.2=2.6\) V: asymmetric, \(V_M>V_{DD}/2\) because the PMOS is relatively strong. Region check at point \(v_I=3.4\) V, \(v_O=2.2\) V (\(V_{TN}=-V_{TP}=0.6\)): NMOS \(V_{GS}=3.4>V_{DS}+V_{TN}=2.8\) → triode; PMOS \(V_{SG}=1.6\), \(V_{SD}=2.8>V_{SG}-|V_{TP}|=1.0\) → saturation.

* Engineering applications
Logic-family compatibility (TTL vs CMOS levels), signal integrity budgets (crosstalk and ground bounce must stay within NM), level shifters, Schmitt triggers for noisy inputs.

* Pitfall
\(V_{OH}\) and \(V_{OL}\) are not \(V_{DD}\) and 0 in ratioed logic (NMOS, pseudo-NMOS): \(V_{OL}\) is set by a current balance and must be computed.
