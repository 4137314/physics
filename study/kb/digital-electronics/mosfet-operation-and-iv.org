#+title: The MOSFET: regions of operation and I–V model
#+summary: A gate voltage above threshold inverts the surface and forms a channel of charge \(C_{ox}(V_{GS}-V_{TN})\); gradual-channel integration gives \(I_D=K_n\left(V_{GS}-V_{TN}-\frac{V_{DS}}2\right)V_{DS}\) in triode and \(I_D=\frac{K_n}2(V_{GS}-V_{TN})^2(1+\lambda V_{DS})\) in saturation, with \(K_n=\mu_nC_{ox}\frac WL\); the source–bulk voltage raises \(V_{TN}\) (body effect).

* Physics
Increasing \(V_{GS}\): accumulation → depletion → *strong inversion* when the surface potential reaches \(2\phi_F\), \(\phi_F=\frac{k_BT}{q}\ln\frac{N_A}{n_i}\). Threshold \(V_{TN}=V_{FB}+2\phi_F+\frac{\sqrt{2q\epsilon_sN_A(2\phi_F+V_{SB})}}{C_{ox}}\) — the last term grows with \(V_{SB}\): body effect \(V_{TN}=V_{TO}+\gamma\left(\sqrt{V_{SB}+2\phi_F}-\sqrt{2\phi_F}\right)\), \(\gamma=\sqrt{2q\epsilon_sN_A}/C_{ox}\).

* Derivation (gradual channel)
Channel charge per area at position \(y\): \(Q_n(y)=-C_{ox}(V_{GS}-V(y)-V_{TN})\) [cite:@shockley1952]. Drift current \(I_D=-W\mu_nQ_n\frac{\dd V}{\dd y}\). Integrate from \(V=0\) (source) to \(V_{DS}\) (drain) over length \(L\):
\[ I_D=\mu_nC_{ox}\frac WL\left(V_{GS}-V_{TN}-\frac{V_{DS}}{2}\right)V_{DS}. \]
Maximum at \(V_{DS}=V_{GS}-V_{TN}\) (channel *pinch-off* at the drain); beyond it the current saturates: \(I_D=\frac{K_n}{2}(V_{GS}-V_{TN})^2\) [cite:@sah1964]. Channel-length modulation adds \((1+\lambda V_{DS})\).

* Regions (NMOS; PMOS with all signs reversed)
| region     | condition                          | I_D                             |
|------------+------------------------------------+---------------------------------|
| cutoff     | V_GS ≤ V_TN                        | 0 (subthreshold leakage)        |
| triode     | V_GS > V_TN and V_DS < V_GS − V_TN | Kn(V_GS − V_TN − V_DS/2)V_DS    |
| saturation | V_GS > V_TN and V_DS ≥ V_GS − V_TN | (Kn/2)(V_GS − V_TN)²(1 + λV_DS) |
Small-signal: \(g_m=\sqrt{2K_nI_D}\), \(r_o=1/\lambda I_D\). For small \(V_{DS}\): \(R_{on}=\frac{1}{K_n(V_{GS}-V_{TN})}\).

* Worked example
Two NMOS in series as an inverter with saturated load: \(K_n=0.5\) mA/V² for both, \(V_{TO}=1\) V, \(V_{DD}=5\) V, \(V_I=3\) V, \(\gamma=0\). Load saturated (\(V_{GS}=V_{DS}\)), switch in triode: \(0.5\left(2-\frac{V_O}2\right)V_O=0.25(4-V_O)^2\Rightarrow V_O^2-6V_O+8=0\Rightarrow V_O=2\) V (4 V violates triode), \(I_D=1\) mA. With \(\gamma=0.5\sqrt{\mathrm V}\), \(2\phi_F=0.6\) V, the load threshold rises with \(V_{SB}=V_O\) and \(V_O\) drops to ≈ 1.65 V (solve numerically): see [[id:nmos-inverters][NMOS inverters]].

* Engineering applications
All digital CMOS, analog amplifiers (current mirrors, differential pairs), power MOSFETs ([[id:power-mosfets-and-igbts][power devices]]), image sensors, flash memory (floating gate shifts \(V_T\)).

* Pitfall
The saturation condition is on \(V_{DS}\) *and* \(V_{GS}\): \(V_{DS}\ge V_{GS}-V_{TN}\). A diode-connected MOS (\(V_{GS}=V_{DS}\)) is always saturated when on — use it to shortcut exam problems.
