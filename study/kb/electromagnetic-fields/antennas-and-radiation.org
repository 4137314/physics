#+title: Antennas and radiation
#+summary: A short dipole \(I\dd l\) radiates \(E_\theta=j\eta\frac{kI\dd l}{4\pi r}\sin\theta\,e^{-jkr}\), power \(P=\frac{\eta\pi}{3}I^2\left(\frac{\dd l}\lambda\right)^2\), radiation resistance \(80\pi^2(\dd l/\lambda)^2\); antennas are characterised by directivity \(D\), gain \(G=e_{rad}D\), aperture \(A_e=\frac{\lambda^2}{4\pi}G\), and links by Friis \(P_r=P_tG_tG_r\left(\frac\lambda{4\pi R}\right)^2\).

* Derivation (Hertzian dipole)
\(A_z=\frac{\mu I\dd l}{4\pi r}e^{-jkr}\) ([[id:potentials-and-gauges][retarded potential]]). \(\mathbf H=\frac1\mu\nabla\times\mathbf A\), \(\mathbf E\) from Ampère. Far field (\(kr\gg1\)) keeps the \(1/r\) terms:
\[ E_\theta=j\eta\frac{kI\dd l}{4\pi r}\sin\theta\,e^{-jkr},\qquad H_\phi=E_\theta/\eta. \]
Integrating \(\braket S=|E_\theta|^2/2\eta\) over the sphere: \(P=\frac{\eta(kI\dd l)^2}{12\pi}\). Directivity \(D=1.5\) (1.76 dBi). The near field (\(1/r^2,1/r^3\)) stores reactive energy. Classical analogue: Larmor \(P=\frac{q^2a^2}{6\pi\epsilon_0c^3}\); the quantum analogue is the dipole [[id:radiative-transitions-and-selection-rules][transition rate]].

* Parameters
| antenna            | D (dBi)       | R_rad       | note                      |
|--------------------+---------------+-------------+---------------------------|
| Hertzian dipole    | 1.76          | 80π²(dl/λ)² | reference; lossy if short |
| half-wave dipole   | 2.15          | 73 Ω        | resonant, easy to match   |
| quarter-wave mono. | 5.15          | 36.5 Ω      | over ground plane         |
| dish, diameter Dd  | (πDd/λ)²·e_ap | –           | e_ap ≈ 0.5–0.7            |
Far-field distance \(2D^2/\lambda\). Arrays: pattern = element × array factor \(AF=\sum I_ne^{jn(kd\cos\theta+\delta)}\) (beam steering by \(\delta\)).

* Worked example: link budget
2.4 GHz Wi-Fi, \(P_t=20\) dBm, \(G_t=G_r=2\) dBi, \(R=100\) m: free-space loss \(20\log(4\pi R/\lambda)=20\log(4\pi\cdot100/0.125)=80.0\) dB. \(P_r=20+2+2-80=-56\) dBm, well above a −90 dBm sensitivity: ~34 dB margin for walls and fading.

* Engineering applications
Mobile and Wi-Fi antennas, phased-array radar and 5G beam-forming, satellite dishes, RFID, radio astronomy, EMC (unintended antennas: cables and slots), optical antennas and grating couplers in [[id:grating-couplers][photonics]].

* Pitfall
Friis holds only in the far field and with matched polarisation and impedance; include mismatch \((1-|\Gamma|^2)\) and polarisation loss factors.
