#+title: Electrostatics and Gauss's law
#+summary: Static charges produce \(\mathbf E=-\nabla V\) with \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf D=\rho\), \(\mathbf D=\epsilon\mathbf E=\epsilon_0\mathbf E+\mathbf P\); Gauss's law \(\oint\mathbf D\cdot\dd\mathbf S=Q_{enc}\) solves symmetric problems in one line, and the stored energy is \(\tfrac12\int\mathbf D\cdot\mathbf E\,\dd V\).

* Statement
Coulomb: \(\mathbf E=\frac{Q}{4\pi\epsilon_0R^2}\hat R\). Superposition and the divergence theorem give
\[ \nabla\cdot\mathbf D=\rho_v,\qquad \nabla\times\mathbf E=0,\qquad \mathbf E=-\nabla V,\qquad V(\mathbf r)=\int\frac{\rho_v\,\dd V'}{4\pi\epsilon|\mathbf r-\mathbf r'|}. \]
In matter, bound charges \(\rho_b=-\nabla\cdot\mathbf P\); for linear media \(\mathbf P=\epsilon_0\chi_e\mathbf E\), \(\epsilon_r=1+\chi_e\).

* Derivation: standard symmetric fields
| source                         | field               |
|--------------------------------+---------------------|
| point charge Q                 | Q/(4πεR²)           |
| infinite line, ρ_l             | ρ_l/(2πεr)          |
| infinite sheet, ρ_s            | ρ_s/(2ε), each side |
| uniform sphere, inside (r < a) | ρ r/(3ε)            |
Each follows by choosing a Gaussian surface on which \(D\) is constant and normal.

* Boundary conditions and energy
Across an interface: \(E_{t1}=E_{t2}\), \(D_{n1}-D_{n2}=\rho_s\). Conductors: \(\mathbf E=0\) inside, surface field \(\rho_s/\epsilon\) normal. Energy \(W=\tfrac12\int\rho V\,\dd V=\tfrac12\int\epsilon E^2\,\dd V\); force by virtual work \(F=-\partial W/\partial x|_Q\).

* Worked example
Gate oxide 2 nm SiO\(_2\) (\(\epsilon_r=3.9\)) at 1 V: \(E=V/d=1/(2\times10^{-9})=5\times10^8\) V/m, i.e. 5 MV/cm, half the breakdown field of SiO\(_2\) (~10 MV/cm). Charge density \(\rho_s=\epsilon E=3.9\cdot8.85\times10^{-12}\cdot5\times10^8=1.7\times10^{-2}\) C/m² ≈ \(1.1\times10^{13}\) electrons/cm² — the inversion charge of a MOSFET ([[id:mosfet-operation-and-iv][MOSFET]]).

* Engineering applications
Capacitive touch screens and MEMS accelerometers, ESD and high-voltage insulation design (field crowding at sharp edges), electrostatic precipitators, depletion regions of [[id:pn-junction-electrostatics][pn junctions]] (Poisson's equation with dopant charge).

* Pitfall
Gauss's law is always true but only *useful* with symmetry: for a finite line or a square plate the field is not constant on any simple surface.
