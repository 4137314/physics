#+title: Magnetostatics: Biot–Savart, Ampère and inductance
#+summary: Steady currents produce \(\mathbf B\) with \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf B=0\) and \(\nabla\times\mathbf H=\mathbf J\), \(\mathbf B=\mu\mathbf H\); Ampère's law \(\oint\mathbf H\cdot\dd\mathbf l=I_{enc}\) gives symmetric fields, magnetic circuits \(\mathcal F=NI=\Phi\mathcal R\) design cores, and inductance \(L=N\Phi/I\) stores energy \(\tfrac12LI^2\).

* Statement
Biot–Savart: \(\dd\mathbf B=\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\frac{I\dd\mathbf l\times\hat R}{R^2}\) [cite:@maxwell1865]. Equivalently \(\mathbf B=\nabla\times\mathbf A\), \(\mathbf A=\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\int\frac{\mathbf J}{R}\dd V'\), giving
\[ \nabla\cdot\mathbf B=0,\qquad \nabla\times\mathbf H=\mathbf J,\qquad \oint_C\mathbf H\cdot\dd\mathbf l=I_{enc}. \]
Force on a current: \(\dd\mathbf F=I\dd\mathbf l\times\mathbf B\); on a charge \(q\mathbf v\times\mathbf B\).

* Standard fields
| source                    | field                      |
|---------------------------+----------------------------|
| infinite wire             | B = μ₀I/(2πr)              |
| long solenoid (n turns/m) | B = μ₀nI inside, 0 outside |
| toroid, N turns           | B = μNI/(2πr)              |
| loop axis, radius a       | μ₀Ia²/(2(a²+z²)^{3/2})     |

* Magnetic circuits
In a core of length \(\ell\), section \(A\), permeability \(\mu\): reluctance \(\mathcal R=\ell/\mu A\); with an air gap \(g\): \(\mathcal R=\frac{\ell}{\mu A}+\frac{g}{\mu_0A}\), usually gap dominated. \(\Phi=NI/\mathcal R\), \(L=N^2/\mathcal R\). Boundary conditions: \(B_{n1}=B_{n2}\), \(H_{t1}-H_{t2}=J_s\). Ferromagnets: hysteresis loop, saturation \(B_{sat}\sim1.5\)–2 T (Fe-Si), core losses.

* Worked example
Ferrite core, \(\ell=5\) cm, \(A=1\) cm², \(\mu_r=2000\), gap 0.5 mm, \(N=50\): \(\mathcal R_{core}=\frac{0.05}{2000\cdot4\pi\times10^{-7}\cdot10^{-4}}=2.0\times10^5\), \(\mathcal R_{gap}=\frac{5\times10^{-4}}{4\pi\times10^{-7}\cdot10^{-4}}=4.0\times10^6\) H\(^{-1}\): \(L=2500/4.2\times10^6=0.6\) mH, nearly independent of the uncertain \(\mu_r\) — that is why power inductors have gaps.

* Engineering applications
Inductors and transformers ([[id:transformer][transformer model]]), motors and actuators, Hall sensors and current clamps, magnetic storage, MRI magnets, on-chip spiral inductors for RF.

* Pitfall
\(\mathbf H\) and \(\mathbf B\) differ inside materials: Ampère's law with free currents uses \(\mathbf H\); flux and force use \(\mathbf B\).
