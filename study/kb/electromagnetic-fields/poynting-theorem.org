#+title: Poynting's theorem and electromagnetic power
#+summary: \(-\oint(\mathbf E\times\mathbf H)\cdot\dd\mathbf S=\frac{\dd}{\dd t}\int\left(\tfrac12\epsilon E^2+\tfrac12\mu H^2\right)\dd V+\int\mathbf J\cdot\mathbf E\,\dd V\): power entering a volume is stored or dissipated; \(\mathbf S=\mathbf E\times\mathbf H\) is the power flux, time average \(\tfrac12\mathrm{Re}(\mathbf E\times\mathbf H^*)\) in phasors.

* Proof
Using \(\nabla\cdot(\mathbf E\times\mathbf H)=\mathbf H\cdot\nabla\times\mathbf E-\mathbf E\cdot\nabla\times\mathbf H\) and Maxwell:
\[ \nabla\cdot(\mathbf E\times\mathbf H)=-\mathbf H\cdot\partial_t\mathbf B-\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf J-\mathbf E\cdot\partial_t\mathbf D=-\partial_t\left(\tfrac12\epsilon E^2+\tfrac12\mu H^2\right)-\mathbf J\cdot\mathbf E. \]
Integrate over \(V\) and apply the divergence theorem.

* Time-harmonic fields
\(\braket{\mathbf S}=\tfrac12\mathrm{Re}(\mathbf E\times\mathbf H^*)\). The complex theorem: \(-\oint\tfrac12\mathbf E\times\mathbf H^*\cdot\dd\mathbf S=P_{loss}+2j\omega(W_m-W_e)\): the imaginary part is reactive power, the same split as \(P+jQ\) in [[id:ac-power-and-power-factor][AC circuits]].

* Worked example: power in a wire
DC current \(I\) in a wire of radius \(a\), resistance per length \(R'\): at the surface \(E=IR'\) along the wire, \(H=I/2\pi a\) around it. \(\mathbf S\) points *radially inward* with magnitude \(EH\); through the surface of length \(\ell\): \(EH\cdot2\pi a\ell=I^2R'\ell\). The energy dissipated in the wire flows in from the surrounding field, not along the copper.

* Plane-wave intensity
In free space \(E/H=\eta_0=377\) Ω, \(I=\frac{E_0^2}{2\eta_0}\). Sunlight 1 kW/m²: \(E_0=\sqrt{2\cdot377\cdot1000}=870\) V/m. Radiation pressure \(I/c=3.3\) µPa (absorbed).

* Engineering applications
Link budgets and power density limits (RF exposure in W/m²), antenna radiated power, laser intensity and damage thresholds, power flow in transmission lines and coax (\(\mathbf S\) concentrated in the dielectric), solar sails.

* Pitfall
\(\mathbf E\times\mathbf H\) is defined up to a curl; only its flux through closed surfaces is unambiguous. A static charge next to a magnet has \(\mathbf S\neq0\) but no net energy flow.
