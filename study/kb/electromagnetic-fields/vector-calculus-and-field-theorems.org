#+title: Vector calculus and the field theorems
#+summary: Gradient, divergence and curl with the theorems \(\oint_S\mathbf F\cdot\dd\mathbf S=\int_V\nabla\cdot\mathbf F\,\dd V\) (Gauss) and \(\oint_C\mathbf F\cdot\dd\mathbf l=\int_S(\nabla\times\mathbf F)\cdot\dd\mathbf S\) (Stokes); Helmholtz: a field is fixed by its divergence and curl, which is why Maxwell's equations specify them.

* Identities
\[ \nabla\times\nabla f=0,\qquad \nabla\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf A)=0,\qquad \nabla\times(\nabla\times\mathbf A)=\nabla(\nabla\cdot\mathbf A)-\nabla^2\mathbf A,\qquad \nabla\cdot(\mathbf E\times\mathbf H)=\mathbf H\cdot\nabla\times\mathbf E-\mathbf E\cdot\nabla\times\mathbf H. \]
Consequences: curl-free ⇔ \(\mathbf F=-\nabla V\) (conservative, path-independent); divergence-free ⇔ \(\mathbf F=\nabla\times\mathbf A\).

* Coordinates
| system      | divergence of F                                               |
|-------------+---------------------------------------------------------------|
| Cartesian   | ∂Fx/∂x + ∂Fy/∂y + ∂Fz/∂z                                      |
| cylindrical | (1/r)∂(rFr)/∂r + (1/r)∂Fφ/∂φ + ∂Fz/∂z                         |
| spherical   | (1/R²)∂(R²FR)/∂R + (1/R sinθ)∂(sinθ Fθ)/∂θ + (1/R sinθ)∂Fφ/∂φ |

* Helmholtz theorem
If \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf F=\rho_s\) and \(\nabla\times\mathbf F=\mathbf J_s\) are given and \(\mathbf F\to0\) fast enough at infinity, then \(\mathbf F=-\nabla\int\frac{\rho_s}{4\pi R}\dd V'+\nabla\times\int\frac{\mathbf J_s}{4\pi R}\dd V'\), uniquely. Proof sketch: the difference of two solutions is curl- and divergence-free, so it is \(\nabla\phi\) with \(\nabla^2\phi=0\) vanishing at infinity, hence zero.

* Worked example
\(\mathbf F=\hat r\,r\) (cylindrical) in a cylinder of radius \(a\), height \(h\): \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf F=\frac1r\partial_r(r^2)=2\), volume integral \(2\pi a^2h\); flux through the side \(a\cdot2\pi ah\), through the caps 0: equal, as Gauss requires.

* Engineering applications
Every field solver (FEM, FDTD) discretises these operators; Stokes underlies Ampère-loop current probes (Rogowski coils) and Faraday induction; divergence underlies charge conservation in circuit simulators (KCL is \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf J=0\) integrated).

* Pitfall
In curvilinear coordinates the unit vectors depend on position: you cannot differentiate components as if Cartesian.
