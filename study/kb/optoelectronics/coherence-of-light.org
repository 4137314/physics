#+title: Temporal and spatial coherence
#+summary: Coherence time \(\Delta t\approx1/\Delta\nu\) and coherence length \(l_c=c\Delta t\approx\lambda^2/\Delta\lambda\) measure how long a wave stays in phase; fringe visibility in an interferometer equals \(|g^{(1)}(\tau)|\), the Fourier transform of the normalised spectrum (Wiener–Khinchin); spatial coherence across a source of angular size \(\theta\) extends over \(\sim\lambda/\theta\).

* Definition
\(g^{(1)}(\tau)=\frac{\braket{E^*(t)E(t+\tau)}}{\braket{|E|^2}}\). Wiener–Khinchin: \(S(\nu)\) and \(g^{(1)}(\tau)\) are a Fourier pair, so \(\Delta\nu\,\Delta t\sim1\). Lorentzian line: \(|g^{(1)}|=e^{-\pi\Delta\nu|\tau|}\), \(\tau_c=1/\pi\Delta\nu\).

* Coherence length (derivation)
\(\Delta\nu=c\Delta\lambda/\lambda^2\Rightarrow l_c=c/\Delta\nu=\lambda^2/\Delta\lambda\). Interference fringes are visible when the path difference \(<l_c\).

| source                  | Δλ or Δν           | l_c      |
|-------------------------+--------------------+----------|
| white light             | ≈ 300 nm           | ≈ 1 µm   |
| LED, 850 nm             | 40 nm              | ≈ 18 µm  |
| Fabry–Perot laser diode | ≈ 1 nm (multimode) | ≈ 2 mm   |
| DFB laser, 1550 nm      | 1 MHz              | ≈ 300 m  |
| stabilised HeNe         | ≈ 1 kHz            | ≈ 300 km |

* Spatial coherence
A source of size \(s\) at distance \(R\) (angle \(\theta=s/R\)) gives coherent illumination over a transverse distance \(l_s\approx\lambda/\theta\) (van Cittert–Zernike: the coherence function is the Fourier transform of the source intensity) [cite:@zernike1938]. The Sun (\(\theta=9.3\) mrad) is spatially coherent over ~60 µm at 550 nm — Young's double slit with sunlight needs a pinhole.

* Worked example
Optical coherence tomography with an SLED at 1310 nm, \(\Delta\lambda=60\) nm: axial resolution \(\frac{2\ln2}{\pi}\frac{\lambda^2}{\Delta\lambda}=0.44\cdot28.6\) µm \(\approx12.6\) µm in air. Low coherence is the *feature* that gives depth resolution.

* Engineering applications
OCT imaging, coherent optical communications (laser linewidth limits phase noise), LiDAR (FMCW needs \(l_c\) longer than twice the range), speckle (high coherence) vs speckle-free LED illumination, holography.

* Exam-style exercise
A Michelson interferometer is lit (a) by a 1550 nm LED with \(\Delta\lambda_{1/2}=50\) nm, (b) by a DFB laser of linewidth 10 MHz. Coherence lengths? For (a), the visibility at 20 µm path difference (Gaussian spectrum)? (c) Over what width is sunlight (angular diameter 0.53°, \(\bar\lambda=0.55\) µm) spatially coherent?
** Solution
(a) \(l_c=\frac{\lambda^2}{\Delta\lambda}=\frac{1.55^2}{0.05}=48\) µm. (b) \(l_c=c/\Delta\nu=30\) m. Visibility for (a): \(x=20/48=0.42\), \(|g|=e^{-(\pi x)^2/4\ln2}=0.54\). (c) \(\approx\lambda/\theta=0.55\) µm\(/9.25\) mrad \(=59\) µm: Young's slits in sunlight must be closer than ≈ 0.06 mm. LEDs give the short coherence used by OCT (axial resolution ≈ \(l_c/2\)).

* Pitfall
Coherence is not monochromaticity alone: a laser with a stable linewidth can still be spatially multimode (poor spatial coherence), and an LED filtered narrowly becomes temporally coherent but stays thermal in its statistics ([[id:photon-statistics-and-glauber-correlations][g2]]).
