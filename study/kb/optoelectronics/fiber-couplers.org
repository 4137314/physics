#+title: Fibre couplers and star couplers
#+summary: In a fused 2×2 coupler the evanescent fields of two cores overlap and power oscillates between them, \(P_2=P_0\sin^2(\kappa z)\); its quality is measured by splitting ratio \(\frac{P_2}{P_1+P_2}\), excess loss \(10\log\frac{P_0}{P_1+P_2}\), insertion loss \(10\log\frac{P_0}{P_{out}}\) and crosstalk (directivity) \(10\log\frac{P_3}{P_0}\); an \(N\times N\) star coupler has an unavoidable splitting loss \(10\log N\) per output plus its excess loss.

* Model
Two single-mode cores are fused and tapered: the modes spread out of the thinned cores and overlap [cite:@kawasaki1981]. Coupled-mode theory ([[id:directional-couplers][directional couplers]]) with coupling coefficient \(\kappa\): \(P_1=P_0\cos^2\kappa z\), \(P_2=P_0\sin^2\kappa z\). Full transfer at the coupling length \(L_c=\frac{\pi}{2\kappa}\); a 3 dB (50:50) coupler stops at \(L_c/2\). \(\kappa\) depends on \(\lambda\): a long coupler that is 0:100 at 1550 nm and 100:0 at 980 nm is the WDM coupler that combines pump and signal in an [[id:optical-amplifiers][EDFA]].

* Performance parameters
| parameter      | definition (dB unless stated)  | typical      |
|----------------+--------------------------------+--------------|
| split ratio    | P2/(P1+P2), in %               | 50% or 10%   |
| excess loss    | 10 log [P0/(P1+P2)]            | < 0.2 dB     |
| insertion loss | 10 log (P0/P_out), per port    | 3.2–3.6 dB   |
| crosstalk      | 10 log (P3/P0)                 | < −50 dB     |
| return loss    | 10 log (P0/P_reflected)        | > 50 dB      |

Insertion loss of a 3 dB coupler is never below 3.01 dB: half the power goes to the other port by design; the *excess* loss is what is really lost.

* Worked examples
1. *Measured coupler*: \(P_0=200\) µW, \(P_1=90\) µW, \(P_2=85\) µW, \(P_3=6.3\) nW. Split ratio \(85/175=48.6\%\); excess loss \(10\log\frac{200}{175}=0.58\) dB; insertion loss 3.47 dB (to port 1) and 3.72 dB (to port 2); crosstalk \(10\log\frac{6.3\times10^{-9}}{2\times10^{-4}}=-45\) dB.
2. *Star couplers*: splitting loss \(10\log N\) = 9.0, 15.1, 18.1 dB for \(N=8\), 32, 64; with 1.5 dB excess, 16.6 dB for a 1×32 splitter — the dominant term in a passive optical network.
3. *PON budget*: OLT transmitter +3 dBm, ONU sensitivity −28 dBm, 1×32 splitter (16.6 dB), 20 km at 0.35 dB/km (7 dB), two connectors (1 dB): total loss 24.6 dB, margin \(3+28-24.6=6.4\) dB ([[id:optical-link-design][link design]]).

* Engineering applications
Taps for power monitoring (99:1), 3 dB couplers in Mach–Zehnder and Sagnac interferometers ([[id:fiber-optic-sensors][fibre gyroscopes]]), pump/signal WDM couplers in EDFAs, 1×N splitters for FTTH passive optical networks, polarisation beam splitters.

* Exam-style exercise
A 1×16 splitter has 1.2 dB excess loss and feeds 16 homes over 10 km of fibre (0.4 dB/km at 1310 nm) with two splices (0.1 dB each) and two connectors (0.5 dB each). The transmitter launches 0 dBm. (a) Power per home. (b) With a −27 dBm receiver, margin? (c) Can the splitter be 1×32 with a 3 dB margin?
** Solution
(a) Splitting \(10\log16=12.0\) dB; total loss \(12.0+1.2+4.0+0.2+1.0=18.4\) dB → −18.4 dBm. (b) Margin 8.6 dB. (c) 1×32 adds 3 dB (plus ≈ 0.3 dB more excess): margin ≈ 5.3 dB > 3 dB: yes.

* Pitfall
In dB everything adds, but the \(10\log N\) splitting loss is *not* excess loss: quoting a 1×32 splitter as "15 dB lossy" is correct for the budget, wrong as a quality figure.
