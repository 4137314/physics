#+title: Three- and four-level lasers: ruby, He–Ne, Nd:YAG
#+summary: Inversion needs a metastable upper level fed by a pump: in a three-level laser (ruby) the lower level is the ground state, so half the atoms must be pumped, \(P_{min}=V\frac{N_0}{2}\frac{h\nu_{13}}{\tau_{sp}}\); in a four-level laser (He–Ne, Nd:YAG) the lower level is empty and threshold is far lower; threshold inversion \(\Delta N_{th}\approx g_{th}\frac{8\pi n^2\nu^2\tau_{sp}\Delta\nu}{c^2}\), photon lifetime \(\tau_{ph}=\frac{n}{c\alpha_t}\), and the number of lasing modes is the gain width over \(c/2nL\).

* Three-level minimum pump (derivation)
All \(N_0\) ions start in \(E_1\) [cite:@maiman1960]. Inversion begins when \(N_2=N_1=N_0/2\): an energy \(V\frac{N_0}{2}h\nu_{13}\) must be absorbed, and it must be supplied faster than \(E_2\) empties, i.e. within \(\tau_{sp}\): \(P_{min}=V\frac{N_0}{2}\frac{h\nu_{13}}{\tau_{sp}}\). In a four-level system \(E_1\) is above the ground state and drains quickly, so any population in \(E_2\) is already an inversion: thresholds are orders of magnitude lower and CW operation is easy.

* The He–Ne laser
A discharge excites He atoms to metastable 2¹S and 2³S levels; collisions transfer the energy resonantly to Ne levels (3s, 2s), which lase to 2p (632.8 nm, also 543, 1152, 3391 nm); 2p empties to 1s, which empties by collisions with the tube walls — hence narrow tubes [cite:@javan1961]. Brewster windows make the output polarised; one mirror is ≈ 99.9%, the output coupler ≈ 99%. Overall efficiency ≈ 0.1%. Gain is small (a few %/m): the cavity must be low loss.

* Worked examples
1. *Ruby*, \(N_0=10^{19}\) cm\(^{-3}\), rod 10 cm × 1 cm diameter (\(V=7.85\) cm³), pump band 2.2 eV, \(\tau_{sp}=3\) ms: \(P_{min}=7.85\cdot5\times10^{18}\cdot2.2\cdot1.6\times10^{-19}/0.003=4.6\) kW — this is why ruby lasers are flash-pumped and pulsed.
2. *He–Ne Doppler width*: Ne mass \(3.35\times10^{-26}\) kg, 400 K: \(\Delta\nu_{1/2}=2\nu_0\sqrt{\frac{2k_BT\ln2}{Mc^2}}=1.51\) GHz (2.0 pm). Tube 40 cm: mode spacing \(c/2L=375\) MHz, ≈ 4 modes under the FWHM; mode number \(m=2L/\lambda=1.26\times10^6\).
3. *Threshold inversion*, same tube, \(R_1=0.95\), \(R_2=1\), negligible scattering: \(g_{th}=\frac{1}{2L}\ln\frac{1}{R_1R_2}=0.064\) m\(^{-1}\); with \(\tau_{sp}=300\) ns: \(\Delta N_{th}=0.064\cdot\frac{8\pi(4.74\times10^{14})^2\cdot3\times10^{-7}\cdot1.51\times10^9}{(3\times10^8)^2}=1.8\times10^{15}\) m\(^{-3}\) — a tiny fraction of the gas density (\(\sim10^{22}\) m\(^{-3}\)).
4. *Photon lifetime*: \(\tau_{ph}=\frac{1}{c\alpha_t}=52\) ns, cavity \(Q=2\pi\nu\tau_{ph}=1.5\times10^8\).

* Other lasers
Argon ion (488/514.5 nm, W-level, water-cooled, efficiency ≈ 0.05%); CO\(_2\) (10.6 µm, vibrational levels, kW, 10–20% efficiency: cutting and welding); Nd:YAG (four-level, 1064 nm, diode- or lamp-pumped, Q-switched for ns pulses, frequency-doubled to 532 nm); Er-doped fibre (the gain medium of [[id:optical-amplifiers][EDFAs]], three-level-like at 1550 nm) [cite:@geusic1964].

* Engineering applications
Alignment, metrology and interferometry (He–Ne, single frequency stabilised to 10\(^{-9}\)), barcode scanners historically, materials processing (CO\(_2\), Nd:YAG, fibre lasers), medicine and spectroscopy, pumping of dye and Ti:sapphire lasers (Ar ion, doubled Nd).

* Exam-style exercise
A He–Ne laser at 632.8 nm has a 30 cm cavity and gain FWHM 1.5 GHz. (a) Mode spacing. (b) How many modes can lase? (c) Cavity length for single-mode operation. (d) Coherence length in both cases.
** Solution
(a) \(c/2L=500\) MHz. (b) \(1.5/0.5=3\), so 3 (sometimes 2–4 depending on where the comb falls). (c) \(c/2L>1.5\) GHz → \(L<10\) cm (or an intracavity etalon). (d) Multimode: \(l_c\approx c/\Delta\nu=20\) cm; single mode with ≈ 1 MHz linewidth: ≈ 300 m ([[id:coherence-of-light][coherence]]).

* Pitfall
"Doppler width" is the width of the *gain*, not of the laser line: each longitudinal mode is far narrower; the laser spectrum is a comb of narrow lines under the Doppler envelope.
