#+title: Holography: recording and reconstructing a wavefront
#+summary: A hologram records the interference \(|O+R|^2=|O|^2+|R|^2+OR^*+O^*R\) of an object wave \(O\) and a coherent reference \(R\); illuminating the developed plate with \(R\) regenerates a term \(\propto|R|^2O\), the object wave itself with amplitude *and* phase, so the image is three-dimensional; the fringe spacing \(\Lambda=\frac{\lambda}{2\sin(\theta/2)}\) demands sub-µm film resolution and a source coherent over the path differences.

* Principle (derivation)
Exposure is proportional to intensity: \(I=|O|^2+|R|^2+OR^*+O^*R\). With a linear response the transmittance is \(t=t_0+\beta I\). Re-illuminate with \(R\): \(tR=(t_0+\beta|O|^2+\beta|R|^2)R+\beta|R|^2O+\beta R^2O^*\). The first group is the attenuated reference (zero order); \(\beta|R|^2O\) is a copy of the object wave (virtual image, seen behind the plate where the object was); \(\beta R^2O^*\) is the conjugate wave (real image). With an off-axis reference (Leith–Upatnieks) the three waves leave at different angles and do not overlap — Gabor's in-line hologram (1948) suffered from that overlap [cite:@gabor1948;@leith1962].

* Worked examples
1. *Fringe spacing* at 633 nm: \(\theta=30^\circ\) between the beams gives \(\Lambda=1.22\) µm (820 lines/mm); \(\theta=60^\circ\): 0.633 µm; counter-propagating beams (reflection hologram, \(\theta=180^\circ\)): \(\lambda/2=0.32\) µm, 3160 lines/mm. Ordinary photographic film resolves ~100 lines/mm: holograms need special emulsions or photopolymers.
2. *Stability*: fringes must not move by more than ≈ \(\Lambda/4\) during exposure. A 1 s exposure tolerates relative drifts of ≈ 0.1 µm: a vibration-isolated table, or a pulsed laser (ns) for living subjects.
3. *Coherence*: an object 20 cm deep needs path differences up to ≈ 40 cm to stay within the coherence length: a single-longitudinal-mode laser (\(l_c=c/\Delta\nu\) of metres), not a multimode He–Ne with \(l_c\approx20\) cm ([[id:coherence-of-light][coherence]]).

* Engineering applications
Security holograms on banknotes (embossed surface relief), holographic interferometry (compare a wavefront before and after loading: fringes map µm deformations), volume holographic gratings and Bragg filters, head-up and AR displays (holographic waveguides), holographic data storage, digital holography (record on a camera, reconstruct numerically) for microscopy.

* Exam-style exercise
Two 532 nm beams at 40° record a transmission hologram. (a) Fringe period. (b) A 650 nm beam reads it: at what angle is the first-order diffraction relative to the reading beam if it hits the grating normally?
** Solution
(a) \(\Lambda=\frac{0.532}{2\sin20^\circ}=0.778\) µm. (b) Grating equation ([[id:diffraction-and-gratings][gratings]]) with normal incidence: \(\sin\theta_1=\frac{0.650}{0.778}=0.836\), \(\theta_1=56.7^\circ\). A thick (volume) hologram would diffract efficiently only at the Bragg angle; reading at another wavelength mismatches it.

* Pitfall
A hologram is not a picture of the object: it stores the interference pattern, whose spacing is set by the beam angle, not by the object's detail; cutting a hologram in pieces keeps the whole scene, with less resolution.
