#+title: Interference and the Fabry–Perot resonator
#+summary: Two waves add as \(I=I_1+I_2+2\sqrt{I_1I_2}\cos\delta\); in a cavity of length \(L\) with mirror reflectance \(R\), multiple reflections give the Airy transmission \(T=\frac{1}{1+F\sin^2(\delta/2)}\), \(F=\frac{4R}{(1-R)^2}\), resonances every FSR \(=c/2nL\), linewidth FSR/\(\mathcal F\) with finesse \(\mathcal F=\frac{\pi\sqrt R}{1-R}\).

* Two-beam interference
\(|E_1+E_2e^{j\delta}|^2\) gives the formula above; visibility \(V=\frac{2\sqrt{I_1I_2}}{I_1+I_2}|g^{(1)}|\), where \(g^{(1)}\) is the [[id:coherence-of-light][degree of coherence]]. Michelson and [[id:mach-zehnder-interferometers][Mach–Zehnder]] interferometers measure \(\delta=2\pi\Delta(nL)/\lambda\).

* Fabry–Perot (derivation)
Field transmitted after \(m\) round trips: \(E_t=E_0t^2\sum_m(r^2e^{-j\delta})^m=\frac{E_0t^2}{1-Re^{-j\delta}}\), with round-trip phase \(\delta=\frac{4\pi nL}{\lambda}\) (normal incidence, lossless mirrors \(|t|^2=1-R\)). Then
\[ \frac{I_t}{I_0}=\frac{(1-R)^2}{(1-R)^2+4R\sin^2(\delta/2)}. \]
Peaks at \(\delta=2\pi m\): \(\nu_m=m\frac{c}{2nL}\) (longitudinal modes). FWHM \(\delta\nu=\mathrm{FSR}/\mathcal F\); quality factor \(Q=\nu/\delta\nu\); photon lifetime \(\tau_{ph}=Q/2\pi\nu\).

* Dielectric (Bragg) mirror
Alternating quarter-wave layers \(n_1,n_2\): all reflections add in phase at \(\lambda_B\). Reflectance after \(N\) pairs \(R=\left(\frac{1-(n_2/n_1)^{2N}}{1+(n_2/n_1)^{2N}}\right)^2\), stop bandwidth \(\frac{\Delta\lambda}{\lambda_B}=\frac4\pi\arcsin\frac{n_1-n_2}{n_1+n_2}\). Basis of VCSEL mirrors, [[id:bragg-gratings][Bragg gratings]] and [[id:photonic-crystals][photonic crystals]].

* Worked example
Semiconductor laser chip, \(L=300\) µm, \(n=3.6\) (\(N_g\approx4\) for mode spacing): FSR \(=c/2N_gL=125\) GHz, i.e. \(\Delta\lambda=\lambda^2/2N_gL=1.0\) nm at 1550 nm. Facet \(R=0.32\): \(\mathcal F=\pi\sqrt{0.32}/0.68=2.6\) — low finesse, which is fine because the gain medium compensates.

* Engineering applications
Laser cavities, wavelength lockers and etalon filters, thin-film filters for DWDM, anti-reflection coatings (quarter-wave layer), Fabry–Perot fibre sensors, VCSEL DBR mirrors.

* Exam-style exercise
A scanning Fabry–Perot spectrum analyser has an air gap of 1 cm and mirrors \(R=0.99\). (a) FSR, finesse, resolution. (b) Can it resolve the modes of a 30 cm He–Ne laser? (c) Light hits a 0.5 mm glass etalon (\(n=1.5\), \(R=0.9\)) at 1550 nm: FSR in nm and number of peaks in the C band (1530–1565 nm).
** Solution
(a) FSR \(=c/2L=15\) GHz, \(\mathcal F=313\), \(\delta\nu=48\) MHz. (b) Modes 500 MHz apart, gain width 1.5 GHz < FSR: yes, about 3 peaks per FSR, each resolved 10× over. (c) FSR \(=\frac{\lambda^2}{2nL}=\frac{1.55^2}{2\cdot1.5\cdot500}\) µm \(=1.6\) nm (200 GHz); \(35/1.6\approx22\) peaks — an etalon of this kind is a DWDM wavelength reference (locker).

* Pitfall
FSR uses the *group* index when computing mode spacing in a dispersive medium; using the phase index gives wrong spacing in semiconductors (error ~10%).
