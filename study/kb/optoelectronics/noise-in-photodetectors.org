#+title: Noise in photodetection and receiver sensitivity
#+summary: Shot noise \(\overline{i_s^2}=2q(I_{ph}+I_d)B\) (times \(M^2F\) in an APD) and thermal noise \(\overline{i_t^2}=4k_BTB/R_L\) set \(\mathrm{SNR}=\frac{(\mathcal RP)^2}{\overline{i_s^2}+\overline{i_t^2}}\); bit error rate \(\mathrm{BER}=\tfrac12\mathrm{erfc}(Q/\sqrt2)\), \(Q=6\) for \(10^{-9}\); NEP is the power giving SNR = 1 in 1 Hz.

* Shot noise (derivation)
Photoelectrons arrive as a Poisson process (coherent light, [[id:coherent-states][Poisson photon statistics]]) of rate \(r=I/q\) [cite:@schottky1918]. Counting in time \(T\): variance \(rT\), so the current variance in bandwidth \(B=1/2T\) is \(\sigma_I^2=\frac{q^2rT}{T^2}=\frac{qI}{T}=2qIB\). With APD gain, each primary carrier yields a random number of secondaries: \(2qIM^2FB\).

* SNR and regimes
\[ \mathrm{SNR}=\frac{(M\mathcal RP)^2}{2q(\mathcal RP+I_d)M^2FB+4k_BTB/R_L}. \]
Thermal-noise limited (pin with low power): SNR ∝ \(P^2\); shot-noise (quantum) limited: SNR \(=\eta P/2h\nu B\) ∝ \(P\). APDs help when thermal noise dominates; optimum \(M\) balances the two [cite:@johnson1928].

* Digital receivers
Gaussian noise, threshold midway: \(Q=\frac{I_1-I_0}{\sigma_1+\sigma_0}\), \(\mathrm{BER}=\frac12\mathrm{erfc}\frac{Q}{\sqrt2}\approx\frac{e^{-Q^2/2}}{Q\sqrt{2\pi}}\). Quantum limit for OOK: 10 photons per bit average (\(e^{-N}=10^{-9}\) with 20 photons per "1").

* NEP and the minimum detectable power
The noise-equivalent power is the optical power that gives SNR = 1 in a 1 Hz bandwidth, in W/√Hz: in a bandwidth \(B\) the minimum power is \(P_{min}=\mathrm{NEP}\sqrt B\), because noise currents grow as \(\sqrt B\). The detector alone (dark current) sets \(P_d=\frac{\sqrt{2qI_dB}}{\mathcal R}\); the load sets \(P_{th}=\frac{1}{\mathcal R}\sqrt{\frac{4k_BTB}{R_L}}\) [cite:@nyquist1928]. Detectivity \(D=1/\mathrm{NEP}\), specific detectivity \(D^*=\sqrt A/\mathrm{NEP}\) (compares detectors of different area).
Numbers: \(\mathcal R=0.95\) A/W, NEP \(2.66\times10^{-15}\) W/√Hz, \(I_d=2\) nA, \(B=10\) GHz, \(R_L=500\) Ω: \(P_{min}=0.27\) nW, \(P_d=2.7\) nW, \(P_{th}=0.61\) µW. The resistor is 2000 times worse than the detector's NEP: a 50–500 Ω load throws away the detector's sensitivity, which is the reason for transimpedance amplifiers and APDs ([[id:exam-basics-of-optoelectronics][solved exam papers]]).

* Worked example
pin receiver, \(\mathcal R=1\) A/W, \(R_L=1\) kΩ (TIA feedback), \(T=300\) K, \(B=7\) GHz (10 Gb/s): thermal \(\sigma_t=\sqrt{4\cdot4.14\times10^{-21}\cdot7\times10^9/1000}=0.34\) µA. For \(Q=6\) with \(\sigma_0\approx\sigma_1\approx0.34\) µA: \(I_1=2\cdot6\cdot0.34=4.1\) µA, i.e. average power ≈ 2 µA/1 A/W = 2 µW = −27 dBm sensitivity (shot noise at 4 µA is only 0.1 µA, negligible).

* Engineering applications
Link budgets (sensitivity in dBm at a target BER), choice pin vs APD vs optical preamplifier, LiDAR range equation, image-sensor SNR (shot-noise-limited pixels), coherent receivers (local oscillator lifts the signal above thermal noise to the shot-noise limit).

* Exam-style exercise
A Si pin photodiode at 850 nm has \(\mathcal R=0.5\) A/W, dark current 5 nA, area 1 mm². (a) NEP if dark-current shot noise dominates. (b) Specific detectivity \(D^*\). (c) Minimum detectable power in a 1 MHz bandwidth. (d) What if it is read with a 100 kΩ resistor at 300 K?
** Solution
(a) \(\mathrm{NEP}=\frac{\sqrt{2qI_d}}{\mathcal R}=\frac{4.0\times10^{-14}}{0.5}=8.0\times10^{-14}\) W/√Hz. (b) \(D^*=\frac{\sqrt A}{\mathrm{NEP}}=\frac{0.1\ \mathrm{cm}}{8\times10^{-14}}=1.25\times10^{12}\) cm √Hz/W. (c) \(8\times10^{-14}\cdot10^3=80\) pW. (d) \(i_{th}=\sqrt{4k_BT/R}=0.41\) pA/√Hz vs dark shot 0.040 pA/√Hz: the resistor dominates by 10×, the effective NEP becomes \(\approx0.82\) pW/√Hz.

* Pitfall
Shot noise is white up to the detector bandwidth; using the bit rate instead of the noise bandwidth \(B\) (≈ 0.7× bit rate for NRZ) misestimates SNR.
