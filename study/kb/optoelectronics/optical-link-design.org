#+title: Optical link design: bit rate, power budget and rise-time budget
#+summary: Dispersion spreads a pulse to rms width \(\sigma\), and two pulses stay distinguishable if \(B\approx\frac{0.25}{\sigma}=\frac{0.59}{\Delta\tau_{1/2}}\) for return-to-zero (twice that for NRZ); independent spreads add in quadrature \(\sigma^2=\sigma_{inter}^2+\sigma_{intra}^2\); a link is designed with a power budget (\(P_{tx}-P_{rx}\ge\sum\) losses + margin, in dB) and a rise-time budget \(t_{sys}=\sqrt{\sum t_i^2}\le\frac{0.7}{B}\) (NRZ); Shannon caps any channel at \(C=W\log_2(1+\mathrm{SNR})\).

* Bit rate and pulse spreading (derivation)
For a Gaussian output pulse of rms width \(\sigma\), the FWHM is \(\Delta\tau_{1/2}=2.355\sigma\). Requiring adjacent RZ pulses, one bit slot \(T=1/B\) apart, to be resolved with \(T\approx4\sigma\) gives \(B=\frac{0.25}{\sigma}=\frac{0.59}{\Delta\tau_{1/2}}\). NRZ uses the full slot for each bit: the same channel carries twice the bit rate. The bit-rate × distance product of a dispersion-limited link is constant because \(\sigma\propto L\).
- Step-index multimode: \(\Delta\tau\approx\frac{Ln_1\Delta}{c}\); a uniform spread of delays has \(\sigma=\frac{\Delta\tau}{2\sqrt3}\).
- Graded index (optimal profile): \(\sigma_{inter}=\frac{Ln_1\Delta^2}{20\sqrt3c}\), a factor \(\Delta/10\) smaller.
- Chromatic: \(\sigma_{intra}=|D|L\sigma_\lambda\) with \(\sigma_\lambda\) the rms source width (\(\Delta\lambda_{1/2}=2.355\sigma_\lambda\)).

* Capacity limits
Nyquist: a channel of bandwidth \(W\) carries at most \(2W\) independent symbols per second. Shannon: with Gaussian noise \(C=W\log_2(1+\mathrm{SNR})\). Example: 10 GHz with SNR 30 dB → \(C=10\log_2(1001)=99.7\) Gb/s, reached only with multilevel coherent formats (QAM). Capacity is multiplied by *time*-division multiplexing (faster electronics), *wavelength*-division multiplexing (DWDM: 80+ channels at 50–100 GHz spacing in the C band, one EDFA amplifies them all), polarisation and space (multicore fibre).

* Worked examples
1. *Step vs graded index*, \(n_1=1.48\), \(\Delta=1\%\), 1 km: step \(\Delta\tau=49.4\) ns, \(\sigma=14.3\) ns, \(B_{RZ}=17.5\) Mb/s. Graded: \(\sigma_{inter}=14.3\) ps (1000× less). With a 1310 nm laser, \(\sigma_\lambda=0.85\) nm and \(|D|=5\) ps/(nm km): \(\sigma_{intra}=4.25\) ps; \(\sigma=\sqrt{14.3^2+4.25^2}=14.9\) ps → \(B_{RZ}=16.8\) Gb/s, \(B_{NRZ}=33.6\) Gb/s over 1 km.
2. *Power budget* at 1310 nm: transmitter 0 dBm, receiver sensitivity −28 dBm, two connectors 0.5 dB each, splices 0.1 dB every 2 km, fibre 0.35 dB/km, margin 3 dB: \(28-1-3=24\) dB available, \(0.35+0.05=0.40\) dB/km → \(L_{max}=60\) km.
3. *Rise-time budget*, 50 km at 1550 nm, \(D=17\) ps/(nm km), \(\Delta\lambda=0.1\) nm: \(t_{disp}=85\) ps; transmitter 100 ps, receiver 150 ps: \(t_{sys}=\sqrt{100^2+85^2+150^2}=199\) ps → \(B_{NRZ}\le0.7/t_{sys}=3.5\) Gb/s.

* Regeneration and amplification
Losses are compensated by optical amplifiers (EDFA every 80–100 km, all channels at once) instead of electronic 3R regenerators (re-amplify, re-shape, re-time, one per channel). Amplifiers do not undo dispersion or noise: those are handled by dispersion management and coherent DSP ([[id:optical-amplifiers][amplifiers]]).

* Engineering applications
Designing datacenter links (multimode, VCSEL, 100 m), access networks ([[id:fiber-couplers][PON]]), metro and long-haul DWDM systems, submarine cables (amplified spans of 50–80 km over 10 000 km).

* Exam-style exercise
An 850 nm LED (\(\Delta\lambda_{1/2}=40\) nm) drives a graded-index fibre (\(n_1=1.47\), \(\Delta=0.01\)) with \(|D_m|=100\) ps/(nm km) at 850 nm. (a) \(\sigma_{inter}\) and \(\sigma_{intra}\) per km. (b) Maximum NRZ bit rate over 2 km. (c) What changes with a VCSEL (\(\Delta\lambda_{1/2}=0.5\) nm)?
** Solution
(a) \(\sigma_{inter}=\frac{1000\cdot1.47\cdot10^{-4}}{20\sqrt3\cdot3\times10^8}=14.1\) ps/km; \(\sigma_\lambda=40/2.355=17\) nm → \(\sigma_{intra}=100\cdot17=1.70\) ns/km. (b) Over 2 km \(\sigma\approx3.40\) ns (chromatic dominates) → \(B_{NRZ}=0.5/3.40\) ns \(=147\) Mb/s. (c) \(\sigma_{intra}=100\cdot0.212\cdot2=42.5\) ps, \(\sigma_{inter}=28.3\) ps, \(\sigma=51\) ps → \(B_{NRZ}\approx9.8\) Gb/s: the narrow source is what makes 10G datacenter links possible.

* Pitfall
Use rms widths (\(\sigma\)) when adding in quadrature and FWHM only with the matching 0.59 factor; mixing them overestimates the bit rate by 2.35×. State RZ or NRZ: they differ by a factor 2.
