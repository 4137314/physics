#+title: Photodetectors: pn, pin and avalanche photodiodes
#+summary: A reverse-biased junction collects photogenerated carriers: responsivity \(\mathcal R=\frac{I_{ph}}{P}=\eta\frac{q\lambda}{hc}=\eta\frac{\lambda[\mu m]}{1.24}\) A/W; a pin diode puts the absorption in a wide depleted i-layer (fast drift); bandwidth is limited by transit time \(f_t\approx0.45v_{sat}/W\) and by \(RC\); an APD multiplies the current by \(M\) at the cost of excess noise \(F(M)\).

* Model
- *Quantum efficiency*: \(\eta=(1-R)\,\zeta\,(1-e^{-\alpha W})\) (surface reflection, collection, absorption in the depleted region).
- *pin*: \(W\) chosen \(\gtrsim1/\alpha\) for absorption but small for speed: trade-off \(\eta\)–bandwidth. Waveguide photodiodes decouple them (light travels along the junction).
- *Bandwidth*: \(\frac1{f_{3dB}^2}\approx\frac{1}{f_t^2}+\frac{1}{f_{RC}^2}\), \(f_{RC}=\frac{1}{2\pi(R_L+R_s)C_j}\), \(C_j=\epsilon A/W\) ([[id:pn-junction-electrostatics][junction capacitance]]).
- *APD*: high-field region gives impact ionisation; mean gain \(M\), excess noise \(F=kM+(1-k)(2-1/M)\), \(k=\beta/\alpha\) ionisation ratio (Si \(k\approx0.02\): low noise; InGaAs/InP \(k\approx0.4\)).

* Worked example
InGaAs pin at 1550 nm, \(\eta=0.8\): \(\mathcal R=0.8\cdot1.55/1.24=1.0\) A/W. Diameter 30 µm, \(W=2\) µm: \(C_j=\frac{13.9\cdot8.85\times10^{-12}\cdot7.07\times10^{-10}}{2\times10^{-6}}=43\) fF; with 50 Ω, \(f_{RC}=74\) GHz; \(f_t=0.45\cdot6.5\times10^4/2\times10^{-6}\approx15\) GHz (holes, \(v\approx6.5\times10^6\) cm/s): transit-limited, ≈ 15 GHz overall → 10–25 Gb/s receivers.

* Engineering applications
Optical receivers (pin + transimpedance amplifier), APDs for long reach and LiDAR, Ge-on-Si waveguide detectors in silicon photonics, image sensors (CMOS pinned photodiodes), single-photon avalanche diodes (SPADs, Geiger mode) for photon counting.

* Exam-style exercise
(a) Cut-off wavelengths of Si (1.12 eV), Ge (0.66 eV), In\(_{0.53}\)Ga\(_{0.47}\)As (0.75 eV). (b) A Si photodiode gives 0.48 A/W at 850 nm: QE? (c) Maximum possible responsivity at 850 nm. (d) The diode is 1 mm² and receives 1 mW/cm²: photocurrent.
** Solution
(a) \(\lambda_g=1.24/E_g\): 1.11, 1.88, 1.65 µm. (b) \(\eta=\mathcal R\frac{1.24}{\lambda}=0.48\cdot1.459=0.70\). (c) \(\eta=1\): 0.69 A/W. (d) \(P=10\) µW → 4.8 µA.

* Pitfall
Responsivity grows with \(\lambda\) at fixed \(\eta\) (more photons per watt) until the cutoff \(\lambda_g\), where \(\eta\) collapses: \(\mathcal R(\lambda)\) is a sawtooth-like curve, not flat.
