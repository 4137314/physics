#+title: Optical absorption and emission in semiconductors
#+summary: Photons with \(h\nu>E_g\) are absorbed across the gap; for direct gaps \(\alpha\propto\sqrt{h\nu-E_g}\) (golden rule × joint density of states), for indirect gaps \(\alpha\propto(h\nu-E_g\mp E_{ph})^2\) and much weaker; the cutoff \(\lambda_g=\frac{1.24}{E_g[\mathrm{eV}]}\) µm sets which materials detect or emit which wavelengths.

* Statement
Absorption \(I(x)=I_0e^{-\alpha x}\), penetration depth \(1/\alpha\) [cite:@roosbroeck1954]. Vertical (\(k\)-conserving) transitions: the photon momentum is negligible, so in a direct gap \(E_c(k)-E_v(k)=h\nu\).

* Derivation (direct gap)
[[id:time-dependent-perturbation-and-golden-rule][Fermi's golden rule]] with a constant momentum matrix element: \(\alpha(h\nu)\propto|p_{cv}|^2\rho_j(h\nu)\). Joint density of states with reduced mass \(\frac1{m_r}=\frac1{m_e}+\frac1{m_h}\): \(\rho_j=\frac{1}{2\pi^2}\left(\frac{2m_r}{\hbar^2}\right)^{3/2}\sqrt{h\nu-E_g}\). Indirect: a phonon of energy \(E_{ph}\) is absorbed or emitted (second order), giving the quadratic edge and \(\alpha\) values 10–100× lower near the edge.

* Worked example
Si at 850 nm: \(\alpha\approx535\) cm\(^{-1}\), \(1/\alpha=19\) µm — a Si photodiode needs a thick absorption region (slow). GaAs at 850 nm: \(\alpha\approx10^4\) cm\(^{-1}\), 1 µm suffices. Ge at 1550 nm: \(\alpha\approx460\) cm\(^{-1}\) (tensile strain raises it), hence Ge-on-Si waveguide photodiodes ([[id:photodetectors][photodetectors]]).

Direct-gap materials (GaAs, InGaAs, Ge above its direct edge) absorb in ≈ 1 µm with a sharp edge; indirect Si needs tens of µm in the near infrared.

* Engineering applications
Choice of detector material for each wavelength band, transparency of Si at 1.3–1.55 µm (why silicon photonics works there), solar-cell absorber thickness, emission colour of LEDs (\(\lambda\approx1.24/E_g\)).

* Exam-style exercise
(a) Thickness of Si that absorbs 90% and 99% of the light entering at 850 nm (\(\alpha=850\) cm\(^{-1}\)). (b) Why is Si a poor choice at 1310 nm? (c) A Ge photodiode at 1550 nm has a 1 µm absorber: fraction absorbed (\(\alpha=460\) cm\(^{-1}\))?
** Solution
(a) \(d=\ln10/\alpha=27\) µm; 99%: 54 µm — thick, slow diodes or resonant/waveguide structures. (b) \(h\nu=0.95\) eV < 1.12 eV: below the gap, \(\alpha\approx0\). (c) \(1-e^{-0.046}=4.5\%\): Ge-on-Si detectors therefore use long waveguide absorbers where light travels tens of µm along the layer.

* Pitfall
Emission in indirect-gap Si is weak not because the gap is "wrong" but because the radiative rate requires a phonon; non-radiative recombination wins. Strained Ge and III-V integration are the workarounds.
