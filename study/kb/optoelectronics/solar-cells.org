#+title: Solar cells
#+summary: A pn junction under light: \(I=I_{ph}-I_0\left(e^{qV/nk_BT}-1\right)\); \(V_{oc}=\frac{nk_BT}{q}\ln\left(1+\frac{I_{sc}}{I_0}\right)\), \(\mathrm{FF}=\frac{V_mI_m}{V_{oc}I_{sc}}\), efficiency \(\eta=\frac{\mathrm{FF}\,V_{oc}I_{sc}}{P_{in}}\); a single junction is limited to ≈ 33% (Shockley–Queisser) by thermalisation and sub-gap losses.

* Model
Superposition of the dark diode and a photocurrent source (generator convention). Equivalent circuit adds \(R_s\) (series) and \(R_{sh}\) (shunt). Photocurrent \(I_{sc}=qA\int\Phi(\lambda)\mathrm{EQE}(\lambda)\dd\lambda\); carriers generated within a diffusion length of the junction are collected ([[id:generation-recombination-and-continuity][diffusion length]]).

* Maximum power point (derivation)
\(P=VI\); \(\frac{\dd P}{\dd V}=0\Rightarrow\left(1+\frac{qV_m}{nk_BT}\right)e^{qV_m/nk_BT}=1+\frac{I_{sc}}{I_0}\). Approximate FF for \(v_{oc}=qV_{oc}/nk_BT\): \(\mathrm{FF}\approx\frac{v_{oc}-\ln(v_{oc}+0.72)}{v_{oc}+1}\).

* Worked example
Si cell 100 cm², \(J_{sc}=35\) mA/cm² (\(I_{sc}=3.5\) A), \(I_0=6\times10^{-12}\) A, \(n=1\): \(V_{oc}=0.0259\ln(5.8\times10^{11})=0.70\) V; \(v_{oc}=27.1\), FF \(=\frac{27.1-\ln27.8}{28.1}=0.85\); \(P_m=0.85\cdot0.70\cdot3.5=2.08\) W; under 1000 W/m² (10 W on 100 cm²): \(\eta\approx21\%\). A series resistance of 10 mΩ dissipates \(I_m^2R_s\approx3.3^2\cdot0.01\approx0.11\) W, about 5% of the output.

A large shunt resistance and a small series resistance keep the I–V curve square; \(R_s\) of a few tenths of an ohm already costs several percent of fill factor in a 3 A cell.

* Engineering applications
Rooftop and utility PV (MPPT trackers in inverters exploit the curve above), space power (multijunction III-V > 30%), perovskite/Si tandems, photovoltaic power converters for optical power delivery, energy harvesting for IoT.

* Exam-style exercise
A 100 cm² Si cell under 1 kW/m² gives \(I_{sc}=3.5\) A, with \(I_0=1\) nA, ideality 1, 300 K. (a) \(V_{oc}\). (b) Maximum power point, fill factor and efficiency. (c) Operating point on a 0.15 Ω load.
** Solution
(a) \(V_{oc}=0.0259\ln(3.5\times10^9)=0.569\) V. (b) Maximising \(V(I_{sc}-I_0e^{V/V_T})\) numerically: \(V_m=0.492\) V, \(I_m=3.32\) A, \(P_m=1.63\) W; FF \(=\frac{1.63}{0.569\cdot3.5}=0.82\); \(\eta=\frac{1.63}{1000\cdot0.01}=16.3\%\). (c) Solve \(I=3.5-10^{-9}e^{V/V_T}\) with \(V=0.15I\): \(V=0.495\) V, \(I=3.30\) A, 1.63 W — the load is almost the optimum one (\(V_m/I_m=0.148\) Ω).

* Pitfall
\(V_{oc}\) grows only logarithmically with light; concentration helps efficiency mainly through \(V_{oc}\), while temperature reduces \(V_{oc}\) by ≈ −2 mV/K (≈ −0.4 %/K for Si modules).
