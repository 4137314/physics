#+title: Directional couplers and coupled-mode theory
#+summary: Two parallel waveguides exchange power through their evanescent fields: with coupling \(\kappa\), \(P_{cross}=\sin^2(\kappa z)\) for identical guides; equivalently the even and odd supermodes beat with \(\Delta n=n_{even}-n_{odd}\), giving cross-over length \(L_x=\frac{\lambda}{2\Delta n}\) and \(\kappa=\pi\Delta n/\lambda\); a 3 dB coupler has \(\kappa L=\pi/4\).

* Coupled-mode equations
\(\frac{\dd a_1}{\dd z}=-j\beta_1a_1-j\kappa a_2\), \(\frac{\dd a_2}{\dd z}=-j\beta_2a_2-j\kappa a_1\). With detuning \(\delta=(\beta_1-\beta_2)/2\) and \(a_1(0)=1\):
\[ P_2(z)=\frac{\kappa^2}{\kappa^2+\delta^2}\sin^2\left(\sqrt{\kappa^2+\delta^2}\,z\right). \]
Complete transfer only for synchronous guides (\(\delta=0\)) — the same algebra as [[id:spin-precession-and-rabi-oscillations][Rabi oscillations]] with \(\kappa\leftrightarrow\Omega/2\), \(\delta\leftrightarrow\Delta/2\).

* Supermode picture
Symmetric and antisymmetric modes of the coupled structure propagate with \(\beta_e\neq\beta_o\); launching into one guide excites both equally; after \(L_x\) they are in antiphase and the power sits in the other guide. Transfer matrix (lossless, symmetric): \(\begin{pmatrix}t&-j\kappa'\\-j\kappa'&t\end{pmatrix}\), \(t=\cos\kappa L\), \(\kappa'=\sin\kappa L\) — a unitary like the [[id:beam-splitter-and-hong-ou-mandel][beam splitter]].

* Worked example
Two 500×220 nm strips with 200 nm gap: \(n_e=2.46\), \(n_o=2.42\) at 1550 nm: \(\Delta n=0.04\), \(L_x=\frac{1.55}{0.08}=19.4\) µm; 3 dB length \(L_x/2=9.7\) µm (plus bend contributions). Wavelength dependence: \(\Delta n\) grows with \(\lambda\) (less confinement), so the split ratio drifts across the C band — broadband alternatives are MMIs and adiabatic couplers.

* Engineering applications
Power taps and splitters, ring-resonator bus coupling, MZI splitters, polarisation splitters (TE/TM couple differently), linear-optical quantum circuits (HOM interference on chip), wavelength-selective couplers.

* Pitfall
Fabrication changes the gap and width, so \(\kappa\) varies exponentially with gap: a ±10 nm gap error can change the splitting ratio by tens of percent.
