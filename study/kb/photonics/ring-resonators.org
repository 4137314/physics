#+title: Ring resonators
#+summary: A ring of length \(L\) coupled to a bus resonates when \(n_{eff}L=m\lambda\); the all-pass transmission \(T=\frac{a^2-2ra\cos\phi+r^2}{1-2ar\cos\phi+(ar)^2}\) drops to zero at critical coupling \(r=a\); \(\mathrm{FSR}=\frac{\lambda^2}{n_gL}\), FWHM \(\approx\frac{(1-ra)\lambda^2}{\pi n_gL\sqrt{ra}}\), \(Q=\lambda/\mathrm{FWHM}\).

* Derivation (all-pass)
Coupler: \(E_t=rE_i-j\kappa E_r\), \(E_{r,out}=-j\kappa E_i+rE_r\) (\(r^2+\kappa^2=1\)); round trip: \(E_r=ae^{-j\phi}E_{r,out}\), \(\phi=\beta L\), \(a\) = single-pass amplitude transmission. Eliminating:
\[ \frac{E_t}{E_i}=\frac{r-ae^{-j\phi}}{1-rae^{-j\phi}}\;\Rightarrow\;T=\frac{a^2-2ra\cos\phi+r^2}{1-2ar\cos\phi+(ar)^2}. \]
At resonance (\(\phi=2\pi m\)): \(T=\frac{(r-a)^2}{(1-ra)^2}\): zero for \(r=a\) (critical coupling: coupling loss equals round-trip loss). \(r<a\): over-coupled; \(r>a\): under-coupled. Add–drop rings route resonant light to a second bus.

* Worked example
Ring radius 10 µm (\(L=62.8\) µm), \(n_g=4.2\), loss 3 dB/cm → \(a=10^{-3\cdot0.00628/20}=0.9978\). FSR \(=\frac{1.55^2}{4.2\cdot62.8}\) µm \(=9.1\) nm. Critically coupled (\(r=a\)): FWHM \(=\frac{(1-a^2)\lambda^2}{\pi n_gL a}=\frac{0.0044\cdot2.4025}{\pi\cdot4.2\cdot62.8}\) µm \(=12.8\) pm, \(Q\approx1.2\times10^5\), photon lifetime \(\tau=Q\lambda/2\pi c\approx100\) ps.

* Engineering applications
Compact modulators (ring modulators, small capacitance), WDM filters and multiplexers, biosensors (resonance shift ∝ analyte index), frequency combs in SiN microrings (Kerr solitons), photon-pair sources, Purcell enhancement ([[id:spontaneous-emission-and-purcell-effect][Purcell]]).

* Pitfall
High Q means extreme sensitivity to temperature: \(\dd\lambda/\dd T\approx\lambda\frac{\dd n/\dd T}{n_g}\approx70\) pm/K in Si, larger than the linewidth above — active thermal stabilisation is mandatory.
