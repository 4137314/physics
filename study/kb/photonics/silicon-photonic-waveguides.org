#+title: Silicon photonic waveguides
#+summary: SOI strip (500×220 nm) and rib waveguides are single-mode at 1550 nm with \(n_{eff}\approx2.4\), \(n_g\approx4.2\); loss of 1–3 dB/cm comes mainly from sidewall roughness (scattering \(\propto\Delta n^2\)); the huge index contrast allows bends of a few µm; compact model \(n_{eff}(\lambda)\approx n_1+n_2(\lambda-\lambda_0)+n_3(\lambda-\lambda_0)^2\).

* Model
| type  | geometry                | features                                |
|-------+-------------------------+-----------------------------------------|
| strip | 500 × 220 nm, full etch | tight bends (2–5 µm), higher loss       |
| rib   | 500 × 220, 90 nm slab   | lower loss, electrical contact (doping) |
| slot  | two rails, 100 nm gap   | field in the gap: sensing, nonlinear    |
| SiN   | 1 µm × 400 nm           | low loss, low contrast, visible light   |
Compact model: \(\beta(\lambda)=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}n_{eff}(\lambda)\), loss \(\alpha\) (field amplitude \(e^{-\alpha L/2}\) for power loss \(\alpha\)) [cite:@soref1986]. \(n_g=n_1-\lambda_0n_2\).

* Loss mechanisms
Sidewall roughness (σ ≈ 1–3 nm): scattering \(\alpha\propto\sigma^2(n_1^2-n_2^2)^2\)/width-dependent field at the wall — wider waveguides and rib geometry reduce it. Bending loss falls exponentially with radius. Two-photon absorption and free-carrier absorption at high power. Substrate leakage if the BOX is too thin.

* Worked example
Delay line for 100 ps: \(L=\frac{c\,\Delta t}{n_g}=\frac{3\times10^8\cdot10^{-10}}{4.2}=7.1\) mm, loss at 2 dB/cm ≈ 1.4 dB. Width sensitivity: \(\dd n_{eff}/\dd w\approx2\times10^{-3}\) nm\(^{-1}\): a 5 nm width error shifts a ring resonance by \(\Delta\lambda=\lambda\frac{\Delta n_{eff}}{n_g}=1550\cdot\frac{0.01}{4.2}\approx3.7\) nm — why thermal tuning is needed.

* Engineering applications
Every PIC component; spiral delay lines; low-loss SiN for LiDAR and biophotonics; slot guides for sensing and hybrid electro-optic polymers.

* Pitfall
\(n_g\), not \(n_{eff}\), sets delays and FSRs; \(n_{eff}\) sets phase and resonance positions. Confusing them is the most common error in PIC design exercises.
