#+title: Thyristors
#+summary: A pnpn structure behaves as two coupled transistors; once triggered, positive feedback (\(\alpha_1+\alpha_2\to1\)) latches it on with very low voltage drop at huge currents, and it turns off only when the current falls below the holding current (line commutation) — or, in GTOs/IGCTs, by extracting gate charge.

* Two-transistor model (derivation)
pnp (\(\alpha_1\)) and npn (\(\alpha_2\)) share the middle layers: \(I_A=\frac{\alpha_2I_G+I_{CO1}+I_{CO2}}{1-(\alpha_1+\alpha_2)}\). The \(\alpha\)'s rise with current; when \(\alpha_1+\alpha_2\to1\) the anode current is limited only by the external circuit: the device latches. Triggers: gate current, \(\dd v/\dd t\) (displacement current through \(C_j\)), overvoltage (breakover), light (LTT).

* Characteristics
Forward blocking and reverse blocking (symmetric); on-state \(V_T\approx1\)–2 V at kA; latching current \(I_L\) to stay on after the gate pulse, holding current \(I_H<I_L\) below which it turns off. Limits: \(\dd i/\dd t\) at turn-on (current crowding near the gate), \(\dd v/\dd t\) in blocking.

* Phase control
In an AC circuit, firing at angle \(\alpha\) after the zero crossing controls the average rectified voltage: \(V_{dc}=\frac{2\sqrt2V_{rms}}{\pi}\cos\alpha\) (full-wave controlled bridge, continuous current). Turn-off occurs naturally at current zero.

* Worked example
Controlled rectifier from 230 V rms, \(\alpha=60^\circ\): \(V_{dc}=\frac{2\sqrt2\cdot230}{\pi}\cdot0.5=104\) V (vs 207 V at \(\alpha=0\)). HVDC valve for 500 kV: with 8 kV devices and margin, ~100 thyristors in series per valve, fired by light.

* Engineering applications
HVDC converter stations, static VAR compensators, soft starters, light dimmers (TRIAC), crowbar protection, pulsed power.

* Pitfall
Without proper snubbers a fast \(\dd v/\dd t\) turns a thyristor on unintentionally; and once on, it cannot be turned off by the gate (except GTO/IGCT).
