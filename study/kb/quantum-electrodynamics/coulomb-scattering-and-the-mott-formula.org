#+title: Coulomb scattering of electrons: the Mott formula
#+summary: First-order scattering of a Dirac electron off a static nucleus gives \(\frac{\dd\sigma}{\dd\Omega}=\frac{Z^2\alpha^2E^2}{4|\mathbf p|^4\sin^4(\theta/2)}\left(1-\beta^2\sin^2\frac\theta2\right)\): Rutherford times a spin factor; nuclear size enters as \(|F(q^2)|^2\) and proton recoil leads to Rosenbluth's formula.

* Derivation
1. External field \(A^0=-\frac{Ze}{4\pi|\mathbf x|}\), \(\tilde A^0(\mathbf q)=-\frac{Ze}{|\mathbf q|^2}\). First-order amplitude \(S_{fi}=-ie\int\bar\psi_f\gamma^\mu\psi_iA_\mu\dd^4x\).
2. Energy conserved (static field), \(\mathbf q=\mathbf p_f-\mathbf p_i\), \(|\mathbf q|=2|\mathbf p|\sin\frac\theta2\).
3. Cross section \(\frac{\dd\sigma}{\dd\Omega}=\frac{Z^2\alpha^2}{|\mathbf q|^4}\cdot4\cdot\frac12\sum_{s,s'}|\bar u_f\gamma^0u_i|^2\).
4. Trace (see [[id:gamma-matrix-traces][traces]]): \(\tfrac12\Tr[\gamma^0(\slashed p_i+m)\gamma^0(\slashed p_f+m)]=2(E^2+\mathbf p_i\cdot\mathbf p_f+m^2)=4E^2\left(1-\beta^2\sin^2\frac\theta2\right)\).
5. Combining:
\[ \frac{\dd\sigma}{\dd\Omega}=\frac{Z^2\alpha^2E^2}{4|\mathbf p|^4\sin^4\frac\theta2}\left(1-\beta^2\sin^2\frac\theta2\right). \]
Non-relativistic limit (\(\beta\to0\), \(E\to m\)): [[id:scattering-and-born-approximation][Rutherford]]. For \(\beta\to1\) backscattering vanishes: helicity conservation.

* Form factors and recoil
Extended charge \(\rho(\mathbf r)\): multiply by \(|F(\mathbf q)|^2\), \(F=\int\rho e^{i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf r}\dd^3r\), \(F\approx1-q^2\braket{r^2}/6\): electron scattering measures nuclear radii (Hofstadter). Scattering off a proton with recoil and magnetic moment gives the Rosenbluth formula with \(G_E,G_M\).

* Worked example
\(E=100\) MeV electrons on gold (\(Z=79\)), \(\theta=30^\circ\): \(q=2p\sin15^\circ=51.8\) MeV \(=0.26\) fm\(^{-1}\); \(q\braket{r^2}^{1/2}=0.26\cdot5.3\approx1.4\) — the form factor already reduces the rate by \(\sim(1-1.4^2/6)^2\approx0.45\): nuclear size is visible.

* Engineering applications
Electron microscopy contrast (high-angle annular dark field ∝ \(Z^{\sim1.7}\)), electron-beam lithography backscatter (proximity effect), Monte Carlo transport of electrons in radiotherapy and in detectors, Rutherford backscattering spectrometry for thin-film analysis.

* Pitfall
\(|\mathbf p|\) and \(E\) are both needed: the relativistic Mott formula has \(E^2/|\mathbf p|^4\), not \(1/E^2\).
