#+title: Quantum electrodynamics: the map of the subject
#+summary: QED is the relativistic quantum theory of electrons, positrons and photons with coupling \(e\gamma^\mu A_\mu\); in the propagator approach every process is a sum of Feynman diagrams built from \(S_F\), \(D_F\) and vertices, giving cross sections as power series in \(\alpha\approx1/137\) that agree with experiment to parts per trillion.

* Overview
| block                       | notes                                               |
|-----------------------------+-----------------------------------------------------|
| relativistic wave equations | four-vectors, Klein–Gordon, Dirac, free solutions   |
| propagators and S matrix    | Green's functions, Feynman propagator, S matrix     |
| processes (order e²)        | Mott, Møller/Bhabha, μ pairs, Compton, annihilation |
| rules                       | Feynman rules in momentum space, traces             |
| radiative corrections       | vacuum polarisation, self-energy, vertex, g−2, Lamb |
| strong fields               | supercritical fields, Schwinger pair creation, E–H  |

* Recipe for an exam problem
1. Draw all diagrams of the lowest order (relative signs for exchanged fermions).
2. Write \(-i\mathcal M\) with the [[id:feynman-rules-of-qed][Feynman rules]].
3. Average over initial spins (\(\tfrac14\)), sum over final ones: turn \(|\mathcal M|^2\) into [[id:gamma-matrix-traces][traces]].
4. Evaluate in the centre-of-mass or lab frame with Mandelstam variables.
5. Insert in the [[id:s-matrix-and-cross-sections][cross-section formula]] and check limits (non-relativistic → Rutherford, \(m\to0\)).

* Engineering applications
QED predicts the electron g-factor (metrology of \(\alpha\)), atomic level shifts used by optical clocks, X-ray and gamma interactions with matter (Compton scattering in medical imaging and radiotherapy dosimetry, pair production in PET detectors and shielding), synchrotron light sources.
