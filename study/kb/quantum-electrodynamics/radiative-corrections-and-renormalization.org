#+title: Radiative corrections and renormalisation
#+summary: At order \(\alpha\) three one-loop insertions appear — vacuum polarisation \(\Pi(q^2)\), electron self-energy \(\Sigma(p)\), vertex \(\Lambda^\mu\); their ultraviolet divergences are absorbed into the physical mass and charge (\(Z_1=Z_2\) by the Ward identity), leaving finite predictions such as the running coupling \(\alpha(q^2)\approx\frac{\alpha}{1-\frac{\alpha}{3\pi}\ln\frac{-q^2}{Am^2}}\).

* The three divergent pieces
- *Vacuum polarisation*: a virtual \(e^+e^-\) loop inside a photon propagator: \(\frac{-ig_{\mu\nu}}{q^2}\to\frac{-ig_{\mu\nu}}{q^2[1-\Pi(q^2)]}\) [cite:@feynman1949]. Gauge invariance forces \(\Pi^{\mu\nu}=(q^2g^{\mu\nu}-q^\mu q^\nu)\Pi(q^2)\), so the photon stays massless.
- *Self-energy* \(\Sigma(p)\): the electron emits and reabsorbs a photon; shifts the mass \(\delta m=\frac{3\alpha}{4\pi}m\ln\frac{\Lambda^2}{m^2}\) (log divergent).
- *Vertex* \(\Lambda^\mu(p',p)\): photon exchanged across the vertex; produces form factors \(F_1(q^2),F_2(q^2)\).

* Regularisation and renormalisation
Regulate (cutoff, Pauli–Villars, dimensional) then define parameters by measurements: \(m\) is the pole of the full propagator; \(e\) is the charge measured at \(q^2\to0\) (Thomson limit). The Ward identity \(\partial\Sigma/\partial p_\mu=-\Lambda^\mu(p,p)\), i.e. \(Z_1=Z_2\), ensures charge renormalisation comes only from vacuum polarisation, \(e^2=Z_3e_0^2\): the same for electrons, muons and protons (charge universality).

* Running coupling (derivation)
Renormalised \(\hat\Pi(q^2)=\Pi(q^2)-\Pi(0)=-\frac{2\alpha}\pi\int_0^1x(1-x)\ln\left(1-\frac{q^2x(1-x)}{m^2}\right)\dd x\) [cite:@tomonaga1946;@schwinger1948b]. For \(-q^2\gg m^2\): \(\hat\Pi\approx\frac\alpha{3\pi}\ln\frac{-q^2}{Am^2}\), \(A=e^{5/3}\). The vacuum screens charge like a dielectric: the effective \(\alpha\) grows at short distance, \(\alpha(M_Z^2)\approx1/128\). For \(-q^2\ll m^2\) one gets the Uehling correction to Coulomb, \(\delta V(\mathbf r)=-\frac{4\alpha^2}{15m^2}\delta^3(\mathbf r)\) (contributes −27 MHz to the hydrogen Lamb shift).

* Infrared divergence
The vertex correction also diverges for soft virtual photons; it cancels against real soft-photon emission (bremsstrahlung) below detector resolution (Bloch–Nordsieck). Only inclusive rates are finite.

* Worked example
Electron–electron scattering at \(\sqrt{-q^2}=10\) GeV with only the electron loop: \(\hat\Pi\approx\frac{1}{3\pi\cdot137}\ln\frac{10^2}{e^{5/3}(5.11\times10^{-4})^2}=7.75\times10^{-4}\cdot(24.0-1.67)=0.0173\): the cross section rises by \(\approx3.5\%\) relative to fixed \(\alpha\) — clearly measured at colliders (muon and quark loops add more).

* Engineering applications
Precision metrology (\(\alpha\) from g−2 and atom recoil), radiative corrections in luminosity and electroweak measurements, and the renormalisation-group idea itself, used in critical phenomena and in effective models of materials.

* Pitfall
"Infinities" are not subtracted arbitrarily: they appear only in relations between bare and measured parameters, which are not observable. Predictions relating observables are finite.
