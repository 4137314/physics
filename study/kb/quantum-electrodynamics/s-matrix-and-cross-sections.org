#+title: The S matrix, cross sections and decay rates
#+summary: \(S_{fi}=\delta_{fi}-i(2\pi)^4\delta^4(P_f-P_i)\prod(\text{norm.})\,\mathcal M_{fi}\); squaring and dividing by flux and time gives \(\dd\sigma=\frac{|\mathcal M|^2}{4\sqrt{(p_1\cdot p_2)^2-m_1^2m_2^2}}\dd\Phi\), and in the CM frame for \(2\to2\): \(\frac{\dd\sigma}{\dd\Omega}=\frac{1}{64\pi^2s}\frac{|\mathbf p_f|}{|\mathbf p_i|}|\mathcal M|^2\).

* Definition
\(S_{fi}=\lim_{t\to\infty}\braket{f|U_I(t,-t)|i}\); unitarity \(S^\dagger S=1\) (probability conservation, optical theorem) [cite:@wheeler1937;@heisenberg1943]. Write \(S=1+iT\) and extract energy–momentum conservation: \(\braket{f|iT|i}=(2\pi)^4\delta^4(\sum p_f-\sum p_i)\,i\mathcal M\), with the invariant amplitude \(\mathcal M\) given by [[id:feynman-rules-of-qed][Feynman rules]].

* Derivation of the cross section
Square: \([(2\pi)^4\delta^4]^2=(2\pi)^4\delta^4\cdot VT\). Divide by time \(T\), by the incident flux \(|\mathbf v_1-\mathbf v_2|/V\) and by the target density \(1/V\); with relativistic normalisation \(2E\) particles per volume:
\[ \dd\sigma=\frac{(2\pi)^4\delta^4(P_f-P_i)}{4\sqrt{(p_1\cdot p_2)^2-m_1^2m_2^2}}\,\overline{|\mathcal M|^2}\prod_f\frac{\dd^3p_f}{(2\pi)^32E_f}. \]
The flux factor is Lorentz invariant (\(4E_1E_2|\mathbf v_1-\mathbf v_2|\) in collinear frames). Decay rate: \(\dd\Gamma=\frac{1}{2M}\overline{|\mathcal M|^2}\dd\Phi\).

* Two-body phase space
In the CM frame, \(\int\dd\Phi_2=\frac{|\mathbf p_f|}{16\pi^2\sqrt s}\int\dd\Omega\), so
\[ \left(\frac{\dd\sigma}{\dd\Omega}\right)_{CM}=\frac{1}{64\pi^2s}\frac{|\mathbf p_f|}{|\mathbf p_i|}\overline{|\mathcal M|^2}. \]

* Worked example
\(e^+e^-\to\mu^+\mu^-\) at high energy: \(\overline{|\mathcal M|^2}=e^4(1+\cos^2\theta)\) gives \(\frac{\dd\sigma}{\dd\Omega}=\frac{\alpha^2}{4s}(1+\cos^2\theta)\), \(\sigma=\frac{4\pi\alpha^2}{3s}=\frac{86.8\text{ nb}}{s/\mathrm{GeV}^2}\). At \(\sqrt s=10\) GeV: 0.87 nb. Unit check with \(0.3894\) mb GeV\(^2\).

* Engineering applications
Cross sections set detector rates (\(R=\sigma\mathcal L\)), radiation shielding and dosimetry (attenuation \(\mu=n\sigma\)), interaction lengths in calorimeters, and photon interaction probabilities in X-ray imaging.

* Pitfall
Average over *initial* spins/polarisations (divide by their number), sum over *final* ones; and for identical final particles divide the total cross section by 2 (or integrate over half the solid angle).
