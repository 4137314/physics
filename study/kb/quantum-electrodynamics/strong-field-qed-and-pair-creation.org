#+title: Strong-field QED: pair creation and the Euler–Heisenberg Lagrangian
#+summary: A static field \(E\) can create \(e^+e^-\) pairs by tunnelling through the gap \(2mc^2\), at a rate \(\propto\exp(-\pi E_c/E)\) with \(E_c=\frac{m^2c^3}{e\hbar}=1.3\times10^{18}\) V/m; integrating out the electron loop gives the Euler–Heisenberg Lagrangian, which makes the vacuum a nonlinear, birefringent medium.

* Schwinger pair creation (WKB estimate)
The vacuum is a filled Dirac sea separated by \(2mc^2\) from the positive continuum [cite:@schwinger1951]. In a field \(E\) the two continua are tilted: an electron must tunnel a distance \(d=2mc^2/eE\). The WKB exponent (see [[id:tunnelling-and-wkb][tunnelling]]) with \(\kappa\sim mc/\hbar\) gives \(\exp(-\pi m^2c^3/e\hbar E)\). Exact (Schwinger 1951):
\[ w=\frac{(eE)^2}{4\pi^3\hbar^2c}\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac1{n^2}e^{-n\pi E_c/E}. \]

* Supercritical atoms
For the Dirac Coulomb problem the \(1s\) level dives into the negative continuum at \(Z\approx173\) (finite nuclear size). An empty \(1s\) state then decays by spontaneous positron emission: the vacuum becomes charged. Heavy-ion collisions (U+U, \(Z_{tot}=184\)) were designed to test it.

* Euler–Heisenberg Lagrangian
For slowly varying fields below \(E_c\) [cite:@sauter1931;@heisenberg1936]:
\[ \mathcal L=\frac{\epsilon_0}{2}(E^2-c^2B^2)+\frac{2\alpha^2\epsilon_0^2\hbar^3}{45m^4c^5}\left[(E^2-c^2B^2)^2+7c^2(\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf B)^2\right]. \]
Consequences: light-by-light scattering (seen in ultraperipheral Pb–Pb collisions at the LHC), vacuum birefringence in strong \(B\) (\(n_\parallel-n_\perp=\frac{3\alpha}{30\pi}(B/B_c)^2\), \(B_c=4.4\times10^9\) T).

* Worked example
The best lasers reach \(I\sim10^{23}\) W/cm\(^2\): \(E=\sqrt{2I/\epsilon_0c}\approx8.7\times10^{14}\) V/m, so \(E/E_c\approx7\times10^{-4}\) and \(e^{-\pi E_c/E}\approx e^{-4700}\): no pairs. Experiments instead collide GeV electrons with the laser: in the electron frame the field is boosted by \(\gamma\sim10^4\), reaching \(\chi=\gamma E/E_c\sim1\).

* Engineering applications
High-power laser facility design (ELI, XFEL), magnetar physics, the Zener breakdown of semiconductor diodes is the solid-state analogue (tunnelling across \(E_g\) in a strong field, see [[id:pn-junction-diode-current][diodes]]).

* Pitfall
The rate is non-perturbative in \(e\) (\(e^{-1/e}\)): no finite order of Feynman diagrams reproduces it.
