#+title: The hydrogen atom
#+summary: For \(V=-e^2/4\pi\epsilon_0r\), \(E_n=-\frac{m e^4}{2(4\pi\epsilon_0)^2\hbar^2n^2}=-\frac{13.6\text{ eV}}{n^2}\), degeneracy \(n^2\) (\(2n^2\) with spin), ground state \(\psi_{100}=e^{-r/a_0}/\sqrt{\pi a_0^3}\), \(a_0=0.529\) Å.

* Statement
With reduced mass \(\mu\) and \(\alpha=e^2/4\pi\epsilon_0\hbar c\approx1/137\) [cite:@bohr1913;@schrodinger1926a]:
\[ E_n=-\frac{\mu c^2\alpha^2}{2n^2},\qquad a_0=\frac{4\pi\epsilon_0\hbar^2}{\mu e^2},\qquad l=0,\dots,n-1,\quad m=-l..l. \]

* Derivation
1. Radial equation with dimensionless \(\rho=2\kappa r\), \(\kappa=\sqrt{-2\mu E}/\hbar\), \(\lambda=1/(\kappa a_0)\): \(u''-\frac{l(l+1)}{\rho^2}u+\left(\frac\lambda\rho-\frac14\right)u=0\).
2. Asymptotics: \(u\sim e^{-\rho/2}\) at infinity, \(u\sim\rho^{l+1}\) at the origin. Write \(u=\rho^{l+1}e^{-\rho/2}w(\rho)\).
3. \(w\) satisfies the confluent hypergeometric (Laguerre) equation; its power series grows like \(e^{\rho}\) unless it terminates. Termination requires \(\lambda=n=n_r+l+1\), an integer.
4. Then \(\kappa=1/(na_0)\) and \(E=-\hbar^2\kappa^2/2\mu=-\hbar^2/(2\mu a_0^2n^2)\).
The "accidental" \(l\)-degeneracy comes from a hidden SO(4) symmetry (the conserved Runge–Lenz vector) [cite:@pauli1926].

* Worked example
H\(\alpha\) (\(3\to2\)): \(\Delta E=13.6(1/4-1/9)=1.89\) eV, \(\lambda=hc/\Delta E=656\) nm. *Hydrogenic donor* in silicon: replace \(\mu\to m^*=0.26m_e\), \(\epsilon_0\to11.7\epsilon_0\): \(E_d\approx13.6\cdot0.26/11.7^2=26\) meV (P in Si: 45 meV with central-cell corrections), \(a^*=a_0\cdot11.7/0.26\approx2.4\) nm. This is why donors are ionised at room temperature ([[id:intrinsic-and-doped-semiconductors][doping]]).

* Engineering applications
Spectroscopy and gas sensing, hydrogen masers (21 cm hyperfine line), shallow dopants and excitons in semiconductors (exciton binding in GaAs ≈ 4 meV), Rydberg-atom electrometers.

* Pitfall
\(n\) counts radial nodes plus \(l\) plus one; the 2s state has one radial node, 2p none.
