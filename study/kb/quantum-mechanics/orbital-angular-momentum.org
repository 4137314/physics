#+title: Orbital angular momentum and spherical harmonics
#+summary: \(\mathbf L=\mathbf x\times\mathbf p\) has integer \(l\); its eigenfunctions are the spherical harmonics \(Y_l^m(\theta,\phi)\propto P_l^m(\cos\theta)e^{im\phi}\), and in a central potential \(\psi=R(r)Y_l^m\) with \(u=rR\) solving a 1D equation with the centrifugal barrier \(\hbar^2l(l+1)/2mr^2\).

* Definition
\(L_i=\epsilon_{ijk}x_jp_k\) satisfies the angular-momentum algebra. In spherical coordinates
\[ L_z=-i\hbar\,\partial_\phi,\qquad \mathbf L^2=-\hbar^2\left[\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\partial_\theta(\sin\theta\,\partial_\theta)+\frac{1}{\sin^2\theta}\partial_\phi^2\right]. \]
Single-valuedness \(e^{im(\phi+2\pi)}=e^{im\phi}\) forces integer \(m\), hence integer \(l\).

* Spherical harmonics
\(Y_0^0=\frac1{\sqrt{4\pi}}\), \(Y_1^0=\sqrt{\frac3{4\pi}}\cos\theta\), \(Y_1^{\pm1}=\mp\sqrt{\frac3{8\pi}}\sin\theta e^{\pm i\phi}\). Orthonormal on the sphere, parity \((-1)^l\) under \(\mathbf x\to-\mathbf x\).

* Central potentials
Since \(p^2=-\hbar^2\nabla^2\) and \(\nabla^2=\frac1r\partial_r^2r-\frac{\mathbf L^2}{\hbar^2r^2}\), writing \(\psi=\frac{u(r)}rY_l^m\):
\[ -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}u''+\left[V(r)+\frac{\hbar^2l(l+1)}{2mr^2}\right]u=Eu,\qquad u(0)=0. \]
The energy does not depend on \(m\): \((2l+1)\)-fold degeneracy from rotational symmetry.

* Worked example
Rigid rotor (diatomic molecule), \(H=\mathbf L^2/2I\): \(E_l=\hbar^2l(l+1)/2I\). For CO, \(B=\hbar^2/2I=0.24\) meV (1.92 cm\(^{-1}\)); the \(l=0\to1\) line at 115 GHz is the radio-astronomy tracer of molecular clouds.

* Engineering applications
Atomic orbitals (s, p, d) behind chemical bonding and semiconductor band edges (s-like conduction band, p-like valence band in GaAs and Si); antenna radiation patterns and multipole expansions use the same \(Y_l^m\); orbital angular momentum of light beams for mode-division multiplexing.

* Pitfall
\(Y_l^m\) for \(m\neq0\) are complex; the real "p_x, p_y" orbitals are combinations of \(m=\pm1\) and are not \(L_z\) eigenstates.
