#+title: Spin precession and Rabi oscillations
#+summary: A spin in a static field precesses at the Larmor frequency \(\omega_0=|g|eB/2m\); adding a transverse field rotating at \(\omega\) produces the Rabi formula \(P_{\downarrow}(t)=\frac{\Omega^2}{\Omega^2+\Delta^2}\sin^2\!\left(\tfrac12\sqrt{\Omega^2+\Delta^2}\,t\right)\), the basis of NMR, MRI and qubit control.

* Precession
\(H=-\boldsymbol\mu\cdot\mathbf B=\omega_0S_z\) with \(\omega_0=|e|B/m_e\) for the electron (\(g=2\)). Heisenberg equations: \(\dot S_x=-\omega_0S_y\), \(\dot S_y=\omega_0S_x\): the expectation \(\braket{\mathbf S}\) rotates about \(\hat z\) at \(\omega_0\). Equivalently \(U(t)=e^{-i\omega_0tS_z/\hbar}\) is a rotation by \(\phi=\omega_0t\). A rotation by \(2\pi\) multiplies a spinor by \(-1\) (verified by neutron interferometry).

* Rabi problem
\(H=\tfrac{\hbar\omega_0}{2}\sigma_z+\hbar\Omega\left(\cos\omega t\,\sigma_x+\sin\omega t\,\sigma_y\right)/2\) (rotating field) [cite:@rabi1937]. Go to the frame rotating at \(\omega\): \(\ket{\tilde\psi}=e^{i\omega t\sigma_z/2}\ket\psi\). The Hamiltonian becomes time independent:
\[ \tilde H=\frac\hbar2\left(\Delta\,\sigma_z+\Omega\,\sigma_x\right),\qquad \Delta=\omega_0-\omega. \]
This is precession about an effective field tilted by \(\tan\theta=\Omega/\Delta\) at frequency \(\Omega_R=\sqrt{\Omega^2+\Delta^2}\). Starting from \(\ket\uparrow\):
\[ P_{\uparrow\to\downarrow}(t)=\frac{\Omega^2}{\Omega^2+\Delta^2}\sin^2\frac{\Omega_Rt}{2}. \]
On resonance (\(\Delta=0\)) the spin flips completely after the "\(\pi\) pulse" \(t=\pi/\Omega\).

* Worked example
MRI at \(B_0=1.5\) T on protons (\(\gamma/2\pi=42.58\) MHz/T): \(f_0=63.9\) MHz. With an RF field \(B_1=10\) µT, \(\Omega=\gamma B_1=2\pi\cdot426\) Hz, so the \(\pi/2\) pulse lasts \(t=\pi/2\Omega=0.59\) ms. A gradient \(G=10\) mT/m shifts \(f_0\) by 426 Hz per mm: frequency encodes position.

* Engineering applications
NMR spectroscopy and MRI; atomic clocks (Ramsey interrogation); single-qubit gates in superconducting, ion and spin qubits ([[id:qubits-and-quantum-gates][qubits]]); ESR. The same mathematics describes the optical two-level atom ([[id:jaynes-cummings-model][Jaynes–Cummings]]).

* Pitfall
A linearly polarised field \(2\Omega\cos\omega t\,\sigma_x\) is the sum of two counter-rotating fields; only the co-rotating one is resonant. Dropping the other is the rotating-wave approximation, valid for \(\Omega\ll\omega_0\).
