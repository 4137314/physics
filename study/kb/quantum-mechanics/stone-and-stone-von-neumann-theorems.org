#+title: Stone's theorem and the Stone–von Neumann theorem
#+summary: Strongly continuous unitary groups \(U(t)\) are exactly \(e^{-itA}\) with \(A\) self-adjoint (Stone), which justifies the Hamiltonian as generator of dynamics; irreducible representations of the Weyl form of \([x,p]=i\hbar\) are all unitarily equivalent to the Schrödinger representation (Stone–von Neumann), so wave mechanics is not a choice.

* Stone's theorem
Let \(U(t)\), \(t\in\mathbb R\), be unitary with \(U(t+s)=U(t)U(s)\) and \(t\mapsto U(t)\psi\) continuous for every \(\psi\). Then there is a unique self-adjoint \(A\) with \(U(t)=e^{-itA}\), defined by the spectral theorem, and
\[ D(A)=\left\{\psi:\lim_{t\to0}\frac{U(t)\psi-\psi}{t}\text{ exists}\right\},\qquad A\psi=i\lim_{t\to0}\frac{U(t)\psi-\psi}{t}. \]
Conversely each self-adjoint \(A\) generates such a group. Hence: unitary time evolution ⇔ self-adjoint \(H\); unitary translations ⇔ self-adjoint \(p\).

* Proof idea
Converse: \(e^{-itA}=\int e^{-it\lambda}\dd P(\lambda)\) is unitary and continuous by dominated convergence. Direct: \(A\) as defined is symmetric; its domain is dense because the smoothed vectors \(\int f(t)U(t)\psi\,\dd t\), \(f\in C_c^\infty\), lie in it; deficiency indices vanish because \(\pm i\) eigenvectors of \(A^*\) would grow like \(e^{\pm t}\) under a unitary group.

* Weyl relations and uniqueness
\([x,p]=i\hbar\) cannot hold for bounded operators (take traces in finite dimension: \(0=i\hbar n\)). The Weyl form avoids domains: with \(U(a)=e^{iap/\hbar}\), \(V(b)=e^{ibx}\),
\[ U(a)V(b)=e^{iab}V(b)U(a). \]
*Stone–von Neumann*: every irreducible strongly continuous representation of these relations (finitely many degrees of freedom) is unitarily equivalent to \(L^2(\mathbb R^n)\) with \(x\) multiplication and \(p=-i\hbar\nabla\). Heisenberg's matrices and Schrödinger's waves are the same theory.

* Worked example
Translations on \(L^2(\mathbb R)\): \((U(a)\psi)(x)=\psi(x+a)\) is strongly continuous; its generator is \(\frac{1}{i}\dd/\dd x\) on the Sobolev space \(H^1\), i.e. \(p/\hbar\). The harmonic-oscillator ladder representation is equivalent to it via Hermite functions.

* Engineering applications
The theorem fails for infinitely many degrees of freedom: quantum fields admit inequivalent representations (different vacua), which is the mathematical root of phase transitions, superconductivity ([[id:superconductivity-and-flux-quantization][BCS]]) and of renormalisation in [[id:radiative-corrections-and-renormalization][QED]].

* Pitfall
Formal commutators on a common domain do not imply the Weyl relations (Nelson's counterexamples); uniqueness needs the exponentiated form.
