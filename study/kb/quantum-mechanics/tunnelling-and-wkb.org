#+title: Tunnelling and the WKB approximation
#+summary: A particle of energy \(E<V_0\) crosses a barrier of width \(a\) with probability \(T=\left[1+\frac{V_0^2\sinh^2\kappa a}{4E(V_0-E)}\right]^{-1}\approx16\frac{E}{V_0}(1-\frac{E}{V_0})e^{-2\kappa a}\); for smooth barriers WKB gives \(T\approx\exp\!\left(-\frac2\hbar\int\sqrt{2m(V-E)}\,\dd x\right)\).

* Statement: rectangular barrier
\(V=V_0\) for \(0<x<a\). Incident from the left: \(\psi=e^{ikx}+re^{-ikx}\); inside \(Ce^{\kappa x}+De^{-\kappa x}\), \(\kappa=\sqrt{2m(V_0-E)}/\hbar\); right \(te^{ikx}\). Matching \(\psi,\psi'\) at both edges (4 equations, 4 unknowns) gives
\[ T=|t|^2=\left[1+\frac{V_0^2\sinh^2(\kappa a)}{4E(V_0-E)}\right]^{-1}. \]
The current ([[id:schrodinger-equation-and-time-evolution][probability current]]) is conserved: \(R+T=1\). For \(E>V_0\), \(\sinh\to\sin\) with real \(k'\): transmission resonances \(T=1\) when \(k'a=n\pi\).

* WKB approximation
Write \(\psi=e^{iS(x)/\hbar}\) and expand \(S=S_0+\hbar S_1+\dots\): \(S_0'^2=2m(E-V)\), \(S_1=\tfrac i2\ln S_0'\). Hence
\[ \psi(x)\approx\frac{C}{\sqrt{p(x)}}\exp\!\left(\pm\frac i\hbar\int^xp(x')\dd x'\right),\qquad p=\sqrt{2m(E-V)}, \]
valid when the local wavelength varies slowly: \(|\dd\lambda/\dd x|\ll1\). Connection formulas at turning points give
- tunnelling: \(T\approx e^{-2\int_{x_1}^{x_2}\kappa(x)\dd x}\);
- Bohr–Sommerfeld: \(\oint p\,\dd x=(n+\tfrac12)h\) for bound states.

* Worked example
Electron, \(V_0-E=1\) eV, \(a=1\) nm: \(\kappa=\sqrt{2\cdot9.11\times10^{-31}\cdot1.6\times10^{-19}}/1.055\times10^{-34}=5.1\) nm\(^{-1}\), \(e^{-2\kappa a}=3.6\times10^{-5}\). Thinning to 0.5 nm gives \(6\times10^{-3}\): two orders of magnitude for half a nanometre. This exponential sensitivity is why the STM resolves single atoms and why gate oxides below \(\sim\)1.5 nm leak.

* Engineering applications
- *Scanning tunnelling microscope*: current \(\propto e^{-2\kappa d}\), ~10× per Å.
- *Flash memory*: Fowler–Nordheim tunnelling through a triangular barrier (WKB) charges the floating gate.
- *Gate leakage* in thin oxides → high-k dielectrics.
- *Tunnel diodes*, resonant tunnelling diodes, [[id:josephson-junction-and-rcsj-model][Josephson junctions]], alpha decay (Gamow factor).

* Pitfall
\(T\approx e^{-2\kappa a}\) uses \(\kappa\) of the *amplitude*; the factor 2 comes from \(|t|^2\). Do not use WKB near turning points or for abrupt barriers thinner than a wavelength.
