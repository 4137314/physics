#+title: The variational method
#+summary: For any normalisable trial state, \(E_0\le\frac{\bra\psi H\ket\psi}{\braket{\psi|\psi}}\); minimising over parameters of \(\ket\psi\) gives an upper bound to the ground energy whose error is second order in the error of the wave function.

* Theorem
Expand \(\ket\psi=\sum c_n\ket n\) in energy eigenkets: \(\bra\psi H\ket\psi=\sum|c_n|^2E_n\ge E_0\sum|c_n|^2\). Equality iff \(\ket\psi\propto\ket0\). If \(\ket\psi=\ket0+\epsilon\ket\delta\), the energy error is \(O(\epsilon^2)\): rough wave functions give good energies.

* Method
1. Choose a family \(\psi(\mathbf r;\alpha,\beta,\dots)\) with the right symmetry and boundary behaviour.
2. Compute \(\bar H(\alpha)=\braket{H}/\braket{\psi|\psi}\).
3. Minimise: \(\partial_\alpha\bar H=0\). The minimum is the best estimate in the family.
Linear combinations \(\psi=\sum c_i\phi_i\) give the generalised eigenproblem \(Hc=ESc\) (Rayleigh–Ritz), the basis of quantum chemistry and of finite elements [cite:@ritz1909].

* Worked example: helium
Trial \(\psi=\frac{Z_e^3}{\pi a_0^3}e^{-Z_e(r_1+r_2)/a_0}\) with effective charge \(Z_e\). Kinetic \(2\cdot Z_e^2\,\mathrm{Ry}\), nuclear \(-2\cdot2\cdot2Z_e\,\mathrm{Ry}\), repulsion \(\frac54Z_e\,\mathrm{Ry}\) (Ry = 13.6 eV):
\[ \bar H=\left(2Z_e^2-8Z_e+\tfrac54Z_e\right)\mathrm{Ry}\;\Rightarrow\;Z_e=\tfrac{27}{16},\quad E\approx-2\left(\tfrac{27}{16}\right)^2\mathrm{Ry}=-77.5\text{ eV}, \]
against the measured \(-78.98\) eV (2% error). Each electron screens the nucleus from the other.

* Engineering applications
Density-functional theory (variational in the electron density) designs semiconductors and catalysts; finite-element solvers for waveguide modes ([[id:effective-index-method][photonic modes]]) minimise a Rayleigh quotient; hydrogen-molecule bonding (LCAO).

* Pitfall
The method bounds only the *lowest* energy of a given symmetry; excited states need trial functions orthogonal to the lower ones.
