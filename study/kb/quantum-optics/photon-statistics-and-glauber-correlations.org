#+title: Photon statistics and Glauber correlation functions
#+summary: Photodetection measures normally ordered correlations: \(g^{(1)}(\tau)=\frac{\braket{E^-(t)E^+(t+\tau)}}{\braket{E^-E^+}}\) gives interference visibility, \(g^{(2)}(\tau)=\frac{\braket{E^-(t)E^-(t+\tau)E^+(t+\tau)E^+(t)}}{\braket{E^-E^+}^2}\) photon-pair statistics; \(g^{(2)}(0)=2\) for thermal, 1 for coherent, \(1-1/n\) for Fock light — values below 1 are non-classical.

* Definitions
Split \(E=E^++E^-\) (positive frequency ∝ \(a\)). A photodetector absorbs photons, so its rate is \(\propto\braket{E^-E^+}\), coincidences \(\propto\braket{E^-E^-E^+E^+}\). Single mode: \(g^{(2)}(0)=\frac{\braket{a^{\dagger2}a^2}}{\braket{a^\dagger a}^2}=1+\frac{\Delta n^2-\bar n}{\bar n^2}\).

* Values
| light             | g2(0)   | statistics       |
|-------------------+---------+------------------|
| thermal (chaotic) | 2       | super-Poissonian |
| coherent (laser)  | 1       | Poissonian       |
| Fock n            | 1 − 1/n | sub-Poissonian   |
| single photon     | 0       | antibunched      |
Classical fields satisfy \(g^{(2)}(0)\ge1\) and \(g^{(2)}(0)\ge g^{(2)}(\tau)\) (Cauchy–Schwarz): antibunching is proof of quantum light.

* Derivation for thermal light
For a Gaussian (chaotic) field, factorising fourth moments gives the Siegert relation \(g^{(2)}(\tau)=1+|g^{(1)}(\tau)|^2\). With a Lorentzian line of width \(\Delta\omega\), \(g^{(1)}=e^{-i\omega_0\tau-\Delta\omega|\tau|/2}\) and \(g^{(2)}(\tau)=1+e^{-\Delta\omega|\tau|}\): photons arrive bunched within the coherence time.

* Worked example
Hanbury Brown–Twiss with a sodium lamp, linewidth 1 GHz: bunching lasts \(\tau_c\sim1/\Delta\nu=1\) ns, so detectors need sub-ns timing. A quantum dot measured with \(g^{(2)}(0)=0.02\) has a multiphoton probability 1% of that of a laser with equal \(\bar n\).

* Engineering applications
Single-photon source certification, stellar interferometry (HBT measured star diameters), fluorescence correlation spectroscopy, laser threshold diagnosis (\(g^{(2)}\) goes from 2 to 1 crossing threshold), LIDAR photon counting.

* Pitfall
\(g^{(1)}\) cannot distinguish a laser from filtered thermal light of the same spectrum; only \(g^{(2)}\) and higher orders reveal the statistics.
