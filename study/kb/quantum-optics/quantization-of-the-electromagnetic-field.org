#+title: Quantisation of the electromagnetic field
#+summary: In Coulomb gauge the free field is a sum of independent modes \(\mathbf k,\lambda\), each an oscillator: \(H=\sum\hbar\omega_k(a^\dagger_{k\lambda}a_{k\lambda}+\tfrac12)\), \(\mathbf E(\mathbf r,t)=i\sum\sqrt{\frac{\hbar\omega_k}{2\epsilon_0V}}\boldsymbol\epsilon_{k\lambda}\left(a_{k\lambda}e^{i(\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf r-\omega t)}-\text{h.c.}\right)\); a photon is one quantum of excitation of a mode.

* Derivation
1. Coulomb gauge \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf A=0\), no sources: \(\Box\mathbf A=0\), \(\mathbf E=-\dot{\mathbf A}\), \(\mathbf B=\nabla\times\mathbf A\).
2. In a box of volume \(V\) with periodic conditions, expand \(\mathbf A=\sum_{\mathbf k,\lambda}\left(\mathcal A_{k\lambda}(t)\boldsymbol\epsilon_{k\lambda}e^{i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf r}+\text{c.c.}\right)\), two transverse polarisations \(\boldsymbol\epsilon\perp\mathbf k\).
3. The energy \(\frac12\int(\epsilon_0E^2+B^2/\mu_0)\dd^3r\) becomes \(\sum_{k\lambda}\frac12(P_{k\lambda}^2+\omega_k^2Q_{k\lambda}^2)\) with real canonical pairs built from \(\mathcal A\): one oscillator per mode.
4. Quantise each oscillator, \([Q,P]=i\hbar\), and introduce \(a_{k\lambda}\) with \([a_{k\lambda},a^\dagger_{k'\lambda'}]=\delta_{kk'}\delta_{\lambda\lambda'}\) [cite:@dirac1927]:
\[ \mathbf A=\sum_{k\lambda}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2\epsilon_0\omega_kV}}\left(a_{k\lambda}\boldsymbol\epsilon_{k\lambda}e^{i(\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf r-\omega_kt)}+\text{h.c.}\right),\qquad H=\sum_{k\lambda}\hbar\omega_k\left(a^\dagger_{k\lambda}a_{k\lambda}+\tfrac12\right). \]
Momentum \(\mathbf P=\sum\hbar\mathbf k\,a^\dagger a\): photons carry \(\hbar\omega\) and \(\hbar\mathbf k\).

* Vacuum fluctuations
\(\bra0\mathbf E\ket0=0\) but \(\bra0E^2\ket0=\sum\hbar\omega_k/2\epsilon_0V\neq0\). The "electric field per photon" \(\mathcal E_1=\sqrt{\hbar\omega/2\epsilon_0V}\) sets the atom–cavity coupling \(g=d\mathcal E_1/\hbar\). Differences of zero-point energy between boundary conditions give the Casimir force \(F/A=-\pi^2\hbar c/240d^4\).

* Worked example
Visible photon (\(\lambda=500\) nm) in a 1 µm\(^3\) microcavity: \(\mathcal E_1=\sqrt{\hbar\omega/2\epsilon_0V}=\sqrt{4\times10^{-19}/(1.8\times10^{-29})}\approx1.5\times10^5\) V/m. With \(d=ea_0\), \(g/2\pi=d\mathcal E_1/h\approx1.9\) GHz: strong coupling becomes possible in small cavities. Casimir pressure at \(d=100\) nm: \(\approx13\) Pa — relevant for MEMS stiction.

* Engineering applications
Shot noise and the standard quantum limit in photodetection, spontaneous emission (vacuum-stimulated) setting LED/laser noise, Casimir forces in MEMS, microwave photons in superconducting resonators.

* Pitfall
A photon is not a localised particle with a wave function in the Schrödinger sense; it is an excitation of a mode, which may be spread over the whole cavity.
