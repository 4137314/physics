#+title: Spontaneous emission (Weisskopf–Wigner) and the Purcell effect
#+summary: Coupling an excited atom to the continuum of vacuum modes gives exponential decay with \(\Gamma=\frac{\omega^3d^2}{3\pi\epsilon_0\hbar c^3}\) and a Lorentzian line of FWHM \(\Gamma\); in a cavity the local density of modes changes and the rate is multiplied by the Purcell factor \(F_P=\frac{3}{4\pi^2}\left(\frac\lambda n\right)^3\frac QV\).

* Derivation (golden rule)
\(\Gamma=\frac{2\pi}\hbar\sum_{k\lambda}|\bra{g,1_{k\lambda}}H_I\ket{e,0}|^2\delta(E)\) with \(|H_{I}|^2=\frac{\hbar\omega}{2\epsilon_0V}|\mathbf d\cdot\boldsymbol\epsilon|^2\). The number of modes per polarisation in \(\dd\omega\,\dd\Omega\) is \(V\omega^2\dd\omega\,\dd\Omega/(2\pi c)^3\), and summing over the two polarisations and all directions gives \(\sum_\lambda\int\dd\Omega\,|\hat{\mathbf d}\cdot\boldsymbol\epsilon_\lambda|^2=8\pi/3\). Collecting factors (the volume \(V\) cancels):
\[ \Gamma=\frac{\omega^3|\mathbf d_{eg}|^2}{3\pi\epsilon_0\hbar c^3}. \]
Weisskopf–Wigner solve \(\dot c_e=-\int_0^tK(t-t')c_e(t')\dd t'\) with a kernel of width \(\sim1/\omega\ll1/\Gamma\) (Markov): \(c_e=e^{-\Gamma t/2-i\delta\omega t}\) [cite:@weisskopf1930]. The Fourier transform of the emitted field is a Lorentzian of FWHM \(\Gamma\); \(\delta\omega\) is a (divergent, renormalised) frequency shift: the [[id:lamb-shift][Lamb shift]].

* Purcell effect
Replace the free-space mode density by a single cavity mode of quality \(Q\) and volume \(V\) (Lorentzian density \(\frac{2}{\pi\Delta\omega_c}\)) [cite:@purcell1946]. For an emitter on resonance, at the field maximum and aligned:
\[ F_P=\frac{\Gamma_{cav}}{\Gamma_0}=\frac{3}{4\pi^2}\left(\frac{\lambda}{n}\right)^3\frac{Q}{V}. \]
Off resonance or in a photonic band gap the emission is suppressed: spontaneous emission is a property of atom *and* environment.

* Worked example
InAs quantum dot in a GaAs micropillar: \(\lambda=930\) nm, \(n=3.5\), \(Q=5000\), \(V=5(\lambda/n)^3\): \(F_P=\frac{3}{4\pi^2}\cdot\frac{5000}{5}=76\). A 1 ns radiative lifetime becomes \(\sim13\) ps, and \(\beta=F_P/(F_P+1)\approx99\%\) of photons go into the cavity mode.

* Engineering applications
Fast single-photon sources, thresholdless nanolasers (\(\beta\to1\)), LED extraction and modulation speed, suppression of spontaneous emission in [[id:photonic-crystals][photonic band-gap]] structures, plasmonic nano-antennas.

* Pitfall
\(\Gamma\) is set by \(|\mathbf d|^2\omega^3\) for a given *local density of optical states*; quoting a lifetime without the environment (refractive index \(n\) multiplies \(\Gamma\) by \(n\) in a homogeneous medium) is incomplete.
