#+title: Effective mass, group velocity and holes
#+summary: A wave packet in band \(E(k)\) moves at \(v_g=\frac1\hbar\frac{\dd E}{\dd k}\) and accelerates as \(\hbar\dot k=F\), so near an extremum it behaves as a free particle of mass \(m^*=\hbar^2/(\dd^2E/\dd k^2)\); an empty state at the top of a full band acts as a positive particle with positive mass: the hole.

* Definition and derivation
1. Packet of Bloch waves around \(k\): \(v_g=\frac{\partial\omega}{\partial k}=\frac1\hbar\frac{\dd E}{\dd k}\).
2. Work done by a force: \(\dd E=Fv_g\dd t=\frac{\dd E}{\dd k}\dd k\Rightarrow\hbar\dot k=F\) (Ehrenfest-like, for slowly varying \(F\)).
3. \(\dot v_g=\frac1\hbar\frac{\dd^2E}{\dd k^2}\dot k=\frac{1}{\hbar^2}\frac{\dd^2E}{\dd k^2}F\equiv F/m^*\). In 3D, \(1/m^*\) is a tensor; Si conduction valleys have \(m_l=0.98\), \(m_t=0.19\) (\(m_e\) units).
Near a band minimum: \(E=E_c+\frac{\hbar^2k^2}{2m^*}\): electrons in a crystal behave as free particles with modified mass, and the periodic potential disappears from the equations.

* Holes
In an almost full band, the current of all electrons equals that of a single positive charge moving with the velocity of the missing electron. At the band top \(\frac{\dd^2E}{\dd k^2}<0\): the missing electron has negative mass and charge \(-e\), the hole has \(m_h^*>0\), charge \(+e\), energy increasing downwards [cite:@heisenberg1931]. Valence bands of Si/GaAs: heavy holes, light holes, split-off band.

* Direct and indirect gaps
Direct (GaAs, InP): conduction minimum and valence maximum at the same \(k\) → efficient light emission. Indirect (Si, Ge): minimum at a different \(k\), so a phonon must supply momentum → weak emission, slow absorption edge ([[id:semiconductor-optical-properties][optical properties]]).

* Worked example
GaAs \(m^*=0.067m_e\): in a field of 1 kV/cm an electron starting at rest gains \(v=eEt/m^*=1.6\times10^{-19}\cdot10^5\cdot10^{-13}/(6.1\times10^{-32})\approx2.6\times10^4\) m/s in 0.1 ps (a typical scattering time). Mobility \(\mu=e\tau/m^*=0.26\) m²/Vs = 2600 cm²/Vs, the right order of real GaAs ([[id:drift-diffusion-and-einstein-relation][drift]]).

* Engineering applications
Low effective mass → high mobility → fast transistors (InGaAs HEMTs); strain engineering of Si channels reduces \(m^*\) and boosts mobility in modern CMOS; hole effective mass sets PMOS sizing ([[id:cmos-inverter][CMOS sizing ratio]]).

* Pitfall
\(m^*\) is not a property of the electron but of the band at that point; far from the extremum the parabolic approximation fails (non-parabolicity, velocity saturation).
